About me

Hi, I'm Eduardo Brandao - a new herpetologist, and someone who really loves snakes. I'm 11 years old and I live in Portugal. It all started when my dad and I began reading snake books together. Then, on a family trip to Florida for the Universal and Disney parks, we went herping for the first time - and I was hooked.

Snakes are my favourite animal. I love their patterns (even if not every snake has an elegant one), the feeling of watching something so truly alive, and the way they show off the beauty of nature I've always loved.

Why I made SnakeList

The idea came to me in Orlando. I wanted one place to tick off every snake I'd ever seen, instead of keeping a checklist in Google Keep. I couldn't find an app that did it the way I wanted – iNaturalist can work as a list if you upload photos, but I wanted a real checklist – so I decided to make my own.

Once I got a laptop, I discovered Claude Code and started building SnakeList with it, which saved me days – even weeks – of coding. I described what SnakeList should be and what I wanted it to do, and it grew from there. The name SnakeList is my own idea, not copied from or inspired by anything else.

My favourite snakes

My favourite snake is the Inland Taipan – for its rarity, its venom, its colour and the remote places it lives. It's at the top of my Want to see list, marked as my favourite.

I also love the Parrot Snake. It's an elegant, fascinating snake – its colours, its size, its eyes, basically everything except the shape of its head.

Snakes I've seen

Honestly, not that many yet – and I don't know the exact number, because I'm still ticking them all off in SnakeList.

What you can do on SnakeList

  • Browse all 4,205 snake species, each with its own page and range map, and a photo for most of them
  • Mark the snakes you've seen, and add your photos, dates and places
  • See where every snake lives on the map and test yourself in the quiz
  • Take a photo with the Camera and get suggestions of which snakes it might be

Get in touch

Have an idea, or spotted something wrong? Use the Report button at the top of SnakeList – I read every message.

Snake photos come from iNaturalist photographers, credited on each species. Read more about SnakeList.
