Painted Bronzeback

Dendrelaphis pictus (Gmelin, 1789)

The Painted Bronzeback is a slender, harmless tree snake of South-east Asian forests.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Yellow side stripe on a bronze-brown body.[1]
  • Black stripe through the eye.[1]

Appearance

Olive or brown above with a yellow stripe along each side, edged below by a dark line; a black stripe through the eye.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Brunei, southern China, Cambodia, Laos, Vietnam and Thailand. Populations from India, Bangladesh and Myanmar are now a separate species.[1,2]

Behaviour

Active by day and lives in trees. Special belly scales let it climb steep, smooth surfaces, and it can even 'jump' across gaps between branches.[1]

Reproduction

Lays eggs.[1]

Who described it

First described by Gmelin in 1789. The brackets around the authority, (Gmelin, 1789), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

pictus: ‘painted’

Painted Bronzeback: ‘Painted’ – its colourful pattern; ‘Bronzeback’ – it has a bronze-coloured back.

Worked out from the Latin and Greek roots and the words in the name; parts SnakeList can’t explain are left out.
