Lichtenstein's Green Racer

Philodryas olfersii (Lichtenstein, 1823)

Lichtenstein's Green Racer is a mildly venomous, bright green snake of South America.[1]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Cerrado, Caatinga, forest and restinga, from sea level to 1,700 m.[1]

Behaviour

Active by day, often climbing but also hunting on the ground.[1]

Diet

Small rodents, lizards, amphibians and birds (especially nestlings), and other snakes almost as big as itself.[1]

Reproduction

Lays 4 to 11 eggs, each about 5 cm long.[1]

Who described it

First described by Lichtenstein in 1823. The brackets around the authority, (Lichtenstein, 1823), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

olfersii: named after a person (the ending -ii means ‘of’ someone)

Lichtenstein's Green Racer: ‘Lichtenstein's’ – named after Martin Lichtenstein, a German zoologist; ‘Green’ – its colour; ‘Racer’ – a name for fast-moving snakes.

Worked out from the Latin and Greek roots and the words in the name; parts SnakeList can’t explain are left out.
