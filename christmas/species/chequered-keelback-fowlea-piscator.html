Chequered Keelback

Fowlea piscator (Schneider, 1799)

The Checkered Keelback, or Asiatic Water Snake, is a very common non-venomous water snake of South and South-east Asia.[1]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

In or near fresh water and rice paddies; active by day and by night.[1]

Behaviour

If cornered it rears up and spreads its neck skin like a cobra's hood. It is not venomous, but its bite is painful. It can even lose part of its tail to escape.[1]

Diet

Mainly fish, frogs and arthropods; sometimes rodents and frog eggs.[1]

Reproduction

Usually lays 30 to 70 eggs (from 4 to 100). The female guards them until they hatch after 60 to 70 days; hatchlings are about 11 cm long.[1]

Who described it

First described by Schneider in 1799. The brackets around the authority, (Schneider, 1799), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

piscator: ‘fisherman’

Chequered Keelback: ‘Keelback’ – its scales have a ridge (a keel) along the middle.
