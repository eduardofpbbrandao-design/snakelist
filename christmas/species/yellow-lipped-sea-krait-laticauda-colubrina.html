Yellow-lipped Sea Krait

Laticauda colubrina (Schneider, 1799)

The Yellow-lipped Sea Krait is a highly venomous sea snake of the tropical Indian and Pacific Oceans. It hunts underwater but comes back onto land to rest, digest and lay eggs.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Black-and-blue bands, yellow lip, paddle tail.[1]

Appearance

Black bands, a black head with a yellow upper lip and snout, and a paddle-shaped tail for swimming.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Coasts of the eastern Indian Ocean and western Pacific, from eastern India and Sri Lanka through South-east Asia to New Guinea, Melanesia, Micronesia and Polynesia, and north to Taiwan and the Ryukyu Islands.[1,2]

Who described it

First described by Schneider in 1799. The brackets around the authority, (Schneider, 1799), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

colubrina: ‘snake-like’

Yellow-lipped Sea Krait: ‘Yellow-lipped’ – for its yellow lips; ‘Sea Krait’ – a krait-like snake that lives in the sea.
