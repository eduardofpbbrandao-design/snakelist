Carpet Python

Morelia spilota (Lacépède, 1804)

The Carpet Python is a large, non-venomous python of Australia and New Guinea. Its patterned skin looks a little like an old carpet. There are several subspecies, including the Diamond Python of south-eastern Australia.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Big python with a triangular head and pits along the lips.[1]
  • Busy, carpet-like pattern.[1]

Appearance

A triangular head with a row of heat-sensing pits along the lips. Colour and pattern vary greatly between areas.[1]

Size

Adults: Usually about 2 m long, but up to about 4 m; females are often bigger and heavier than males.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Most of mainland Australia except the dry centre, and southern New Guinea (Indonesia and Papua New Guinea).[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

A wide range of habitats, from rainforest in north-eastern Queensland to river woodlands and forests elsewhere.[1]

Behaviour

Can be active by day or night and often climbs trees, but also lives on the ground and basks in open spots.[1]

Diet

Kills its prey by squeezing (constriction): mainly small mammals, birds and lizards.[1]

Reproduction

Lays 10 to 50 eggs. The mother coils around them and twitches her muscles to warm them until they hatch.[1]
