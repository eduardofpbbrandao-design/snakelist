Colombian Boa

Boa imperator Daudin, 1803

The Central American Boa (or Northern Boa) is a large, heavy, non-venomous boa from Mexico to north-western South America. It was long counted as part of the Boa Constrictor, and is one of the most popular pet snakes in the world.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Large, heavy boa with dark saddles.[1]

Appearance

Its looks vary a lot from place to place; like other boas it has a patterned body with darker saddles and a strong, heavy build.[1]

Size

Adults: Usually about 1.3 to 2.5 m long and around 6 kg, but up to 3.7 m; females are much larger than males.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Mexico, Central America (Guatemala, Belize, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Panama), and Colombia and Ecuador west of the Andes, plus some small islands.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Prefers rainforest, which gives it humidity, warmth, cover and plenty of prey.[1]

Behaviour

Lives alone and is most active at dawn and dusk. Young boas climb into trees and shrubs, but adults are heavier and spend more time on the ground. It strikes if it feels threatened; a big boa's bite hurts but is not venomous.[1]

Diet

Kills by squeezing (constriction). Eats mainly mammals - from rats and squirrels to opossums, agoutis, coatis and even monkeys - and birds.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young.[2]

Where the name comes from

Boa: from a Latin word for a large serpent mentioned in Pliny the Elder's Natural History; its deeper origin is unknown[3]

imperator: ‘emperor’

Colombian Boa: ‘Colombian’ – it lives in Colombia; ‘Boa’ – from boa, a Latin word for a large snake.
