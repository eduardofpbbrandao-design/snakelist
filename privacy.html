Privacy policy

Last updated 11 November 2026

SnakeList (snakelist.com) is a free snake checklist, map and quiz made by Eduardo Brandao. This page explains what SnakeList keeps on your device, what reaches other services when you use it, and how to remove it.

What stays on your device

Your lists (Seen, Want to see, Following, favourites), the notes, dates, places and photos you add to sightings, your settings, quiz progress and achievements are saved in your own browser on your own device. If you make more than one account on a device, each one's list is kept separately on that device. SnakeList has no server of its own: unless you make a SnakeList account (see below), your list is only on your device, so the creator can't see it.

Cookies and visit counting

On snakelist.com, SnakeList uses Google Analytics to count visits and see which parts are used. The first time you visit, SnakeList asks about cookies:

  • Accept cookies – Google Analytics may set cookies on your device to count visits. SnakeList also remembers, on your device only, which snakes and searches you look at, so “Picked for you” can put snakes you're likely to enjoy first. Those interests are not sent anywhere.
  • Only essentials – Google Analytics is told not to use analytics cookies, and SnakeList doesn't remember what you look at. Google may still receive basic, cookieless information that a page was opened (Google calls this consent mode), as it does from any page you load.

Google handles that information under Google's privacy policy. To change your choice, clear SnakeList's site data in your browser and choose again. The copy of SnakeList on claude.ai has no analytics.

Sign in with Google

Signing in is optional. If you choose to sign in with Google, SnakeList asks Google for your name, email address and profile picture (to show who is signed in), and for access to a private app-data folder in your own Google Drive. Your list is saved there as one small file so it can sync between your devices. SnakeList can only see the file it made in that folder – not your other Drive files – and the file stays in your Google account: the creator has no copy of it and no access to it.

You can sign out at any time. To remove the synced file and SnakeList's access, go to myaccount.google.com/permissions and remove SnakeList.

SnakeList's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

SnakeList accounts (email and password)

You can also make a SnakeList account with an email address and a password. These accounts run on Google Firebase. Firebase Authentication keeps your email address, the name you type and a scrambled (hashed) form of your password – never the password itself. Your list (seen, want to see, followed, dates and places) is saved in a Firebase database so it can sync between your devices. The database rules only let you, while signed in, read or change your own list; nobody else using SnakeList can see it. As the owner of the Firebase project the creator could technically open it, but does not look at people's lists. Your own photos stay on your device and are not uploaded with an email account.

To delete your SnakeList account and its saved list, email the creator (see Contact below) from the address you signed up with.

Trail cameras you add to the map

With a SnakeList account you can add a trail camera to the map's Cameras layer. What you fill in – its title, its place (rounded to about 1 km), the snake photos and video links you add, and your name only if you tick “Show my name” – is saved in SnakeList's Firebase database. A new camera is only shown to everyone after the creator has checked and approved it. You can edit or delete your camera and its photos on the map at any time.

Camera and location

  • Snake photos you identify are looked at by a recogniser that runs inside your browser. The photo is not uploaded.
  • Your location is only used when you allow it (or pick a spot on the map). For the camera, the spot is sent to iNaturalist to find out which snakes are recorded near it, so the suggestions only include snakes from your area. When you log where you saw a snake, the place is saved on your device with your sighting.

Other services SnakeList loads from

Some parts of SnakeList are loaded from other services while you use it. Your browser asks them directly, so like any website they receive your IP address and basic browser information, plus what is needed for the request, and handle it under their own privacy policies:

  • iNaturalist – snake photos, sightings (people's shared observations) in the part of the map you're looking at, conservation status and nearby species. The request includes the species or map area concerned.
  • Wikipedia – the short description on species pages.
  • Wikidata – on species pages, details about the person who first described the snake (the request names the species).
  • Google Translate – only if you use the language button: the page's text is sent to Google to be translated.
  • Map imagery – satellite and street map tiles (Esri/ArcGIS and map tile providers) and the Leaflet map library (from cdnjs/Cloudflare) when you switch map style.
  • Google Fonts – the lettering SnakeList uses.
  • GitHub Pages – hosts snakelist.com itself.

Reports and photos you send

The Report button and “send a photo” open your own email app with a message addressed to the creator. Nothing is sent until you press send in your email app; then it reaches the creator's inbox like any other email, with your email address.

Children

SnakeList doesn't ask for personal information to use it, and signing in is optional.

Removing your data

  • Your lists and settings: clear SnakeList's site data in your browser, or uninstall the installed app. (This deletes your list on that device, so sign in or use “Download a backup” first if you want to keep a copy.)
  • A trail camera you added: delete it on the map, or email the creator.
  • Your SnakeList account (email and password): email the creator from that address and it will be deleted with its list.
  • Your Google Drive copy and SnakeList's access: myaccount.google.com/permissions.
