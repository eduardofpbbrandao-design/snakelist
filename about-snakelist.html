What SnakeList is

SnakeList is a checklist of all 4,254 snake species in the world, from the tiniest blind snakes to the biggest pythons. You can mark the snakes you've seen (with the date, the place and your own photos), keep a list of the ones you want to see, and follow your favourites. It works on a computer, a tablet or a phone, and you can install it like an app.

The checklistEvery species with a photo, its family, where it lives, whether it's venomous, and filters to find any snake.
The mapWhere each snake lives, down to states, provinces and islands, a heatmap of how many species live in each place, live sightings and habitats.
Species pagesA page for every snake, with its photo, facts, range map, the states and cities where it's found, and related snakes.
The quizEasy, medium and hard rounds: where does this snake live, which state, and snake facts.
The cameraTake a photo of a snake and SnakeList suggests which species it might be, from the snakes that live where you are - on your own device.
AchievementsBadges for the snakes you see and the quizzes you finish.

Why it was made

SnakeList was made by Eduardo Brandao, a new herpetologist who loves snakes. He wanted one place to tick off every snake he had ever seen, instead of keeping a checklist in Google Keep. When he found out that no app like that existed - iNaturalist can work as a list if you add photos, but he wanted a real checklist - he decided to make his own.

The name SnakeList is his own idea.

When

  • The idea - on a family trip to Florida, in Orlando, after going herping for the first time.
  • 21 August 2026 - the first SnakeList: a checklist of the world's snakes to tick off.
  • Since then - more than a hundred updates: photos for thousands of species, the map, the quiz, the camera, species pages, sign-in to keep your list on every device, and much more. Every change is listed in SnakeList itself (the clock button at the top).

How it's made

Eduardo builds SnakeList with Claude Code, describing what SnakeList should do and checking every change. The information comes from open sources:

  • Snake photos and live sightings from iNaturalist photographers, credited on each species
  • Country, state and island outlines from Natural Earth (public domain)
  • Cities from GeoNames
  • Fossil snake ages from the published fossil record

Habitats are estimated from where each snake lives and what kind of snake it is, so they're a guide rather than a survey.

Free, and your list is yours

SnakeList is free. Your list is saved on your own device, and if you sign in, in your own account - see the privacy policy.
