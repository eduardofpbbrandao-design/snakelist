Rosy Boa

Lichanura trivirgata Cope, 1861

The Rosy Boa is a small, calm boa of the deserts of the south-western United States and north-western Mexico. It is one of only a few boas native to the mainland United States.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Three long stripes down the body.[1]
  • Small, smooth and fairly thick-bodied.[1]

Appearance

Usually three stripes (orange, tan, brown or pink) run down a pale body - 'trivirgata' means 'three-striped'. Colours vary a lot from place to place; some have a pink, rosy belly.[1]

Size

Adults: A small boa: even a big adult's body is only about as thick as a golf ball.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Southern California and south-western Arizona in the USA, and Baja California and western Sonora in Mexico.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Rocky deserts - most often granite outcrops. Where there are few rocks it hides in rodent burrows.[1]

Behaviour

Spends most of its life hidden under rocks and in cracks, away from heat and predators.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young, about six at a time; the babies can be up to 30 cm long.[1]

Where the name comes from

trivirgata: ‘three-striped’, from Latin roots: tri- ‘three’ + -virgata ‘striped’

Rosy Boa: ‘Boa’ – from boa, a Latin word for a large snake.
