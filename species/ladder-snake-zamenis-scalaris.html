Ladder Snake

Zamenis scalaris (Schinz, 1822)

The Ladder Snake is a non-venomous rat snake found only in south-western Europe, common in Portugal and Spain. It is named after the ladder-like pattern on the backs of young snakes - scalaris is Latin for "of a ladder".[1]

Identification

Appearance

Adults: Yellowish to dark brown, with two darker stripes running along the body from the neck to the tail.[1]

Variation

Young: Juveniles are brighter, yellow to light brown, with the black "ladder" pattern along the back. The pattern fades with age, leaving the simpler two-striped adult pattern.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: South-western Europe: the Iberian Peninsula and the Mediterranean south of France.[1]

Diet

A study of 368 snakes in Spain found that adults feed almost only on warm-blooded prey - mainly mammals, nearly 95% of the diet by weight - with a few birds; adults climb trees and buildings to raid nests. Young snakes eat small lizards, baby rodents, spiders and grasshoppers.[1,2]

Did you know?

  • Only young Ladder Snakes show the ladder pattern that gives the species its name.[1]
