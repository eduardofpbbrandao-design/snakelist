Sidewinder

Crotalus cerastes Hallowell, 1854

The Sidewinder is a small rattlesnake of the sandy deserts of the south-western United States and north-western Mexico. It is named for its sideways way of moving, called sidewinding. It is also called the Horned Rattlesnake.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Sandy flats with creosote bush and mesquite, and occasionally rocky slopes.[2]

Behaviour

Active mainly from March to October. It often buries itself in sand to wait for prey, and may twitch its tail as a lure - a habit adults lose as they switch from lizards to rodents.[1,2]

Diet

Lizards, pocket mice, kangaroo rats and other small mammals; in many areas lizards make up most of the diet.[1,2]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young, on average eight to ten (other sources give 5 to 18).[2,3]
