Corn Snake

Pantherophis guttatus

The Corn Snake, also called the Red Rat Snake, is a colourful, harmless constrictor of the south-eastern and south-central United States. It is one of the most popular pet snakes in the world, and many colour forms have been bred in captivity.[1,2]

Ecology

Habitat

Forest edges and woodland, fields, swamps and hardwood hammocks; it also turns up in barns and abandoned buildings, where it hunts rodents.[3,6]

Diet

A constrictor that squeezes its prey. Adults eat mostly rodents and other small mammals, plus birds and their eggs; young snakes also take lizards, small snakes and frogs.[1]

Reproduction

Females lay about 10 to 30 eggs in early summer, which hatch from July to September. The parents give no care to the eggs or young.[4,7]

Conservation

LCListed as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List, with a stable population.[1,3]

Common and not threatened over most of its range.[1]
