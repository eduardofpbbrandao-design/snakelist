Racer

Coluber constrictor Linnaeus, 1758

The North American Racer is a fast, slender, non-venomous snake found across much of the United States and into Canada, Mexico, Guatemala and Belize. Despite its scientific name 'constrictor', it does not usually squeeze its prey.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Long, slender and fast-moving.[1]
  • Usually one plain colour (often black, blue or brown).[1]

Appearance

Most are a single plain colour, which gives the subspecies their names: black, brown, tan, blue or green racers.[1]

Size

Adults: Usually 50 to 152 cm depending on the subspecies; the record is 185.4 cm.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Much of the United States on both sides of the Rocky Mountains, north into southern Canada and south into Mexico, Guatemala and Belize.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Usually open, grassy places, but also brush, swamps, roadsides and suburbs; in Florida it is the commonest snake in neighbourhoods.[1]

Behaviour

Fast and very active by day. It hunts mostly on the ground but climbs well and may raid birds' nests.[1]

Diet

Small rodents and other mammals, frogs, toads, lizards, small turtles, birds and other snakes; young ones eat insects too.[1]

Reproduction

Lays eggs.[2]
