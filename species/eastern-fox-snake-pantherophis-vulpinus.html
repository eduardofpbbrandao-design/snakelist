Eastern Fox Snake

Pantherophis vulpinus (Baird & Girard, 1853)

The Eastern Fox Snake is a harmless rat snake of the upper Midwest of the United States and southern Ontario.[1]

Identification

Appearance

Light golden brown with dark brown spots, a yellow checkerboard belly and a short, flattened snout.[1]

Size

Adults: About 3 to 6 ft (0.9 to 1.8 m) long.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: East of the Mississippi River: Wisconsin, Illinois, eastern Missouri, Indiana, Michigan and Ohio, and southern Ontario. Its close relative, the Western Fox Snake, lives west of the river.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Open woodland, prairie, farmland, pasture and marshes.[1]

Behaviour

A strong climber, but usually on the ground. Active by day, sometimes hunting at night in hot summers.[1]

Reproduction

Lays eggs.[2]

Who described it

First described by Baird & Girard in 1853. The brackets around the authority, (Baird & Girard, 1853), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

Pantherophis: ‘panther snake’, from the Greek roots panther- ‘panther’ + -ophis ‘snake’

vulpinus: ‘fox-like’

Eastern Fox Snake: ‘Eastern’ – where it lives (in the east).

Worked out from the Latin and Greek roots and the words in the name; parts SnakeList can’t explain are left out.
