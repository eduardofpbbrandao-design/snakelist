Diamondback Water Snake

Nerodia rhombifer (Hallowell, 1852)

The Diamondback Water Snake is a common, non-venomous water snake of the central United States and eastern Mexico. It is often mistaken for the venomous Cottonmouth, but it is harmless unless picked up or bothered.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Usually seen near slow water.[1]

Appearance

Brown or olive with a black net-like pattern of rough diamond shapes down the back, dark bars on the sides and a yellowish belly with black blotches. Its scales are strongly ridged, so it feels rough.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: The central United States, mainly along the Mississippi valley (Texas and Oklahoma east to Alabama and north to Iowa and Illinois), and eastern Mexico south to Chiapas, Guatemala and Belize.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Near slow-moving water: streams, rivers, ponds and swamps.[1]

Behaviour

It hangs from branches over the water and dips its head under to catch fish. It often basks on branches and drops into the water when someone comes near. If cornered it hisses and flattens its head and body to look bigger; it bites only if grabbed.[1]

Diet

Mainly fish, caught by dipping its head into the water from a branch.[1]

Reproduction

Mates in spring and gives birth to live young in late summer or early autumn; the babies are about 20 to 25 cm long. Females have been known to have young without a male.[1,2]

Did you know?

  • A female Diamondback Water Snake can sometimes have babies without a father (parthenogenesis).[1,2]
