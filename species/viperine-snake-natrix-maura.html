Viperine Snake

Natrix maura (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Viperine Snake is a small, harmless water snake of south-western Europe and north-western Africa. It spends much of its life in and around rivers, ponds and streams, hunting fish and frogs. Its zigzag pattern, triangular-looking head and bad temper make many people mistake it for a viper - which is how it got its name - but it has no dangerous venom.[1,2]

Overview

Did you know?

  • Its name means it pretends to be a viper - it copies a viper's looks and behaviour, but it is harmless.[1]

Who described it

First described by Linnaeus in 1758. The brackets in its name’s authority, (Linnaeus, 1758), mean it was first described under a different genus.

Identification

Appearance

Grey, brown or reddish above, with a dark zigzag stripe along the middle of the back and a row of dark eye-spots with yellowish centres along each side. Viper-like markings and a head that can look triangular are the reason it is confused with vipers.[1]

Size

Adults: A relatively small snake, reaching up to about 80 cm in total length.[2]

Similar species

  • Iberian Grass Snake Natrix astreptophora – the other Natrix of Iberia; it has reddish eyes and lacks the Viperine Snake's dark zigzag along the back.[5,1]

Range

Published distribution

Native: Portugal, Spain (including Gibraltar), France, south-western Switzerland and north-western Italy, plus Sardinia, and in North Africa Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia and Libya. SnakeList's map below currently shows only its European countries.[6,1]

Introduced: Mallorca and Menorca in the Balearic Islands. A genetic study suggests it reached Mallorca from south-eastern France in Roman times, so after some 2,000 years the Balearic authorities treat it as part of the islands' fauna rather than as a modern invasive snake.[7,8,2]

Ecology

Habitat

Closely tied to fresh water: rivers, streams, ponds, lakes, irrigation channels and other wetlands, where it hunts in the water and basks on the banks.[2,1]

Behaviour

When threatened on land it hisses and strikes in a viper-like way, but usually does not actually bite.[1]

Diet

Mainly fish and amphibians, caught in the water. On Mallorca it preys on Perez's Frog, among other animals.[1,2]

Reproduction

Lays eggs. In one study, females did not lay bigger or more frequent clutches when food was more plentiful, unlike many other snakes.[1,9]

Conservation

LCListed as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List.[3]

Not threatened overall. As a water snake it depends on clean rivers and wetlands.[3,1]

On Mallorca: Predation by viperine snakes is believed to be one of the causes of the decline of the rare Mallorcan Midwife Toad, and removing snakes has been tried at some toad sites.[10,11]
