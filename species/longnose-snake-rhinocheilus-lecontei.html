Longnose Snake

Rhinocheilus lecontei Baird & Girard, 1853

The Long-nosed Snake is a shy, harmless burrowing snake of the south-western United States and northern Mexico, named for its long, slightly upturned snout.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Black saddles with cream speckles, between red patches.[1]

Appearance

Black and red saddles on a yellow or cream background - a little like a coral snake - with cream spots inside the black saddles. It has a long, slightly upturned snout.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: South-western USA (California and Idaho east to Kansas, Oklahoma and Texas) and northern Mexico.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Desert, grassland, shrubland and savanna.[1]

Behaviour

Active at night and spends most of its time buried underground.[1]

Diet

Lizards, amphibians and sometimes smaller snakes; rarely rodents.[1]

Reproduction

Lays 4 to 9 eggs in early summer, which hatch in late summer or early autumn.[1]
