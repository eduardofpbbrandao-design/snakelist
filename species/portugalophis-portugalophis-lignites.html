Extinct · fossil snake · Late Jurassic

Portugalophis

Portugalophis lignites Caldwell et al., 2015

Portugalophis lignites is an extinct reptile from the Late Jurassic of Portugal, known only from pieces of its jaws. It was described in 2015 as one of the oldest known snakes, but a 2025 study of its group, the parviraptorids, left open whether they were snakes at all.[1,2]

Little is known about this species. Only a few published studies describe it, so this page gives just what they record and says “Not known” for the rest.

Fossils and geological context

What has been found

Two upper jaw bones (maxillae) and one lower jaw bone (dentary), from coal beds (lignites) in Portugal, which gave the species its name.[1,3]

Find site: The Guimarota coal mine near Leiria.[4]

Geological age and setting

Late Jurassic (Kimmeridgian), about 155 million years ago.[3,4]

Size estimate

Not known: jaw fragments alone do not give a reliable body length.[1]

What is uncertain

When it was described, it was placed among the earliest snakes. Parviraptorids had been known only from very incomplete remains; a much more complete skeleton from the Isle of Skye, described in 2025, showed a mix of snake-like jaws and teeth with a lizard-like body. Its authors placed parviraptorids in their own family and left open whether they are early relatives of snakes or more primitive squamates that evolved snake-like jaws independently.[2,5]
