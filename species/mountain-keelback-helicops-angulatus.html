The Brown-banded Water Snake is an aquatic snake of tropical South America and Trinidad and Tobago.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Banded water snake, usually seen in water.[1]

Appearance

Olive or grey-brown with dark brown, black-edged crossbands.[1]

Size

Adults: Up to about 78 cm, though one female of 113 cm has been reported.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Venezuela, Colombia, the Guianas, Brazil, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia and Trinidad.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Fresh and brackish water.[1]

Diet

Mainly fish, and probably frogs, tadpoles and eggs.[1]

Reproduction

One of only a handful of snakes known to both lay eggs and give birth to live young.[2]

Did you know?

  • The same species can either lay eggs or give birth to live babies.[2]

Who described it

First described by Linnaeus in 1758. The brackets around the authority, (Linnaeus, 1758), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

angulatus: ‘angled’

Mountain Keelback: ‘Mountain’ – it lives in mountains; ‘Keelback’ – its scales have a ridge (a keel) along the middle.

Worked out from the Latin and Greek roots and the words in the name; parts SnakeList can’t explain are left out.
