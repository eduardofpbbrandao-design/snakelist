Western Foxsnake

Pantherophis ramspotti Crother et al., 2011

The Western Fox Snake is a harmless rat snake of the upper Midwest of the United States, west of the Mississippi River. It was named as a separate species in 2011.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Forest, shrubland, grassland and wetlands, and also pasture, old fields and farmland.[1]

Behaviour

Fairly bold. When confronted it shakes its tail like a rattlesnake, takes an S-shaped pose and snaps.[1]

Diet

A constrictor that eats mainly mice and other small rodents, plus young rabbits, frogs and eggs.[1]

Reproduction

Lays eggs in rotting stumps and under logs.[1]

Who described it

Where the name comes from

Pantherophis: ‘panther snake’, from the Greek roots panther- ‘panther’ + -ophis ‘snake’

ramspotti: named after a person called Ramspott

Western Foxsnake: ‘Western’ – where it lives (in the west).
