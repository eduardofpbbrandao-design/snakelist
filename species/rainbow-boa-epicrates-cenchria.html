Rainbow Boa

Epicrates cenchria (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Brazilian Rainbow Boa is a medium-sized, non-venomous boa of South America, named for the rainbow-coloured shimmer that plays across its scales in sunlight. It is a popular snake in zoos and the pet trade.[1,2]

Identification

Appearance

Orange to reddish-brown with a row of large dark rings along the back, each with a paler centre, and dark spots along the sides. The scales are smooth, and microscopic ridges on their surface split light into colours, producing the iridescent sheen.[1,2]

Size

Adults: Adults are commonly reported at about 1.5-2 m, with females usually larger than males. They become mature at around 1.5 m.[1,3]

Newborns: About 38-50 cm long at birth.[2]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Much of tropical South America, especially the Amazon basin and the Guianas, south to Bolivia and east into Brazil's Atlantic coastal region.[1,4]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Mainly humid tropical forest and woodland; it is also found in more open, savanna-like country in parts of its range.[1,3]

Diet

A constrictor that feeds mainly on warm-blooded prey. Rodents are the prey most often reported; birds and lizards are also eaten.[1,3]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young, which develop inside the mother. Sources disagree widely on litter size, giving ranges anywhere from about 2 to 35 young.[1,2,3]

Did you know?

  • Its rainbow sheen is not made by pigment: tiny ridges on each scale bend the light, like the colours on a soap bubble.[1,2]

Who described it

First described by Linnaeus in 1758. The brackets around the authority, (Linnaeus, 1758), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Classification notes

Rainbow boas were long treated as one species, Epicrates cenchria, with nine subspecies. In 2008 Paulo Passos and Ronaldo Fernandes revised the group using scales, skeleton and reproductive organs and recognised five species: E. cenchria, E. maurus, E. crassus, E. alvarezi and E. assisi. The Brazilian Rainbow Boa is the true E. cenchria.[4,5]
