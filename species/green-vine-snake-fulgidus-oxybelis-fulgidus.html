Green Vine Snake (fulgidus)

Oxybelis fulgidus (Daudin, 1803)

The Green Vine Snake is a very slender, rear-fanged tree snake of Central America and northern South America.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Very long, thin green body and pointed head.[1]

Appearance

Very thin (about 2 cm thick), with a long, delicate tail and a long, very pointed head.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Southern Mexico through Central America to Colombia, Venezuela, the Guianas, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia and Brazil.[1,2]

Behaviour

Lives almost only in trees, watching from branches for small animals below.[1]

Diet

Rodents, lizards, frogs, birds and other small animals, which it follows a short way before striking.[1]

Reproduction

Lays eggs.[2]

Who described it

Where the name comes from

fulgidus: ‘shining’

Green Vine Snake (fulgidus): ‘Green’ – its colour.
