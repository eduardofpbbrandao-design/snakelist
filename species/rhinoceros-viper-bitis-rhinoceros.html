Rhinoceros Viper

Bitis rhinoceros (Schlegel, 1855)

The Rhinoceros Viper, or West African Gaboon Viper, is a large, heavy venomous viper of the West African forests. It gets its name from the pair of horn-like scales on the tip of its nose.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • The dark triangle behind the eye is a single, undivided mark.[1]

Appearance

Two enlarged scales on the nose stand up like little horns. It is very like the Gaboon Viper, but the dark triangle running back from its eye towards the corner of the mouth is not split in two.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: West Africa: Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Ivory Coast, Ghana and Togo.[2]

Who described it

First described by Schlegel in 1855. The brackets around the authority, (Schlegel, 1855), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

rhinoceros: ‘rhinoceros’

Rhinoceros Viper: ‘Viper’ – from the Latin vipera, ‘bearing live young’ - most vipers give birth instead of laying eggs.
