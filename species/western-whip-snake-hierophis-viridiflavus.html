Western Whip Snake

Hierophis viridiflavus (Lacépède, 1789)

The Western (or Green) Whip Snake is a fast, slender snake of south-western Europe. It is usually counted as harmless, although it has small grooved teeth at the back of the mouth.[1]

Identification

Appearance

Slender with a small head, big eyes with round pupils and smooth scales. Greenish-yellow with heavy dark green or black bands, especially towards the front; the tail has thin stripes. In parts of Italy and further east many are almost completely black. Young ones are greyish.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: North-eastern Spain, Andorra, France (with Corsica), southern Switzerland, Italy (with Sardinia and Elba), Malta, Slovenia and coastal Croatia.[2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Forests, shrubland, Mediterranean scrub, farmland, pastures, rural gardens and even towns.[1]

Behaviour

Bites: A man who was bitten for several minutes had some effects on his nerves and muscles, and the snake has been found to have small rear fangs linked to a gland - so it should not be handled.[1]

Reproduction

Lays eggs.[2]

Who described it

First described by Lacépède in 1789. The brackets around the authority, (Lacépède, 1789), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

Hierophis: ‘sacred snake’, from the Greek roots hier- ‘sacred’ + -ophis ‘snake’

viridiflavus: ‘green and yellow’

Western Whip Snake: ‘Western’ – where it lives (in the west); ‘Whip Snake’ – it is long and thin, like a whip.
