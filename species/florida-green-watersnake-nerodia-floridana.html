Florida Green Watersnake

Nerodia floridana (Goff, 1936)

The Florida Green Watersnake is a harmless snake of the south-eastern United States and the largest water snake in North America.[1]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Calm, weedy water such as swamps and marshes; rarely rivers or streams.[1]

Behaviour

Active all year in southern Florida; further north it hibernates and basks on sunny winter days.[1]

Diet

Mainly fish such as sunfish, crappies and small bass, plus frogs, tadpoles and salamanders.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young.[1]

Who described it

First described by Goff in 1936. The brackets around the authority, (Goff, 1936), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

floridana: ‘of Florida’ – named after where it lives

Florida Green Watersnake: ‘Florida’ – after Florida; ‘Green’ – its colour; ‘Watersnake’ – it lives in or near water.
