Smooth Snake

Coronella austriaca

The Smooth Snake is a fairly small colubrid snake found from Portugal to Iran. In Great Britain it is the rarest snake, living only on lowland heathland in the south of England, where it is a strictly protected species.[1,2,3]

Identification

Appearance

A dark line runs from the eye along the side of the head, giving it a masked look.[5]

Size

Adults: Both sexes average about 60 to 75 cm.[4]

Similar species

  • European Adder Vipera berus – the Adder; the Adder has a bold zigzag down its back, while the Smooth Snake has a dark stripe through the eye instead.[5,6]

Range

Published distribution

Native: Widespread in the western Palearctic, from Portugal to Iran. In Britain most populations are in Dorset and Hampshire, with smaller ones in Surrey, West Sussex and Berkshire, plus some reintroduced populations.[1,3]

Ecology

Habitat

In Britain (regional): Confined to lowland heathland; the area of heath is stable, but its quality for smooth snakes continues to decline.[2,7]

Diet

An ambush hunter. Small reptiles - especially lizards and slow worms - are the main prey in diet studies; small mammals are also taken.[4,2]

Reproduction

Ovoviviparous: the eggs hatch inside the mother, and the young are born live.[2]

Conservation

LCLeast Concern on the global IUCN Red List, but rated Endangered in Great Britain.[1]

Protected in the UK as a European protected species. Heathland management, including conservation grazing, is studied to help it.[3,8]
