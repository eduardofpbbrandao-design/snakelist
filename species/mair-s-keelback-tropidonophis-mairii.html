Mair's Keelback

Tropidonophis mairii (Gray, 1841)

Mair's (Common) Keelback is a harmless water snake of northern and eastern Australia and New Guinea. It is one of the few Australian snakes that can eat small cane toads without being poisoned.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Keeled scales and dark spots, near fresh water.[1]

Appearance

Olive, brown or blackish with small black spots or crossbars; strongly ridged (keeled) scales. It looks like the venomous Rough-scaled Snake.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Northern and eastern Australia (Western Australia to New South Wales), southern Papua New Guinea and Indonesian New Guinea.[1,2]

Where the name comes from

mairii: named after a person called Mair

Mair's Keelback: ‘Mair's’ – named after a person called Mair; ‘Keelback’ – its scales have a ridge (a keel) along the middle.
