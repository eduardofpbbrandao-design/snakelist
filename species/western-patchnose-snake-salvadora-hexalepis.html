Western Patchnose Snake

Salvadora hexalepis (Cope, 1867)

The Western Patch-nosed Snake is a harmless snake of the deserts of the south-western United States and northern Mexico, with a big curved scale over its nose.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Patch-like curved scale on the tip of the nose.[1]
  • Yellowish with dark side stripes.[1]

Appearance

A thick scale curves back over the top of the snout like a patch. Yellowish with blackish stripes along the sides.[1]

Size

Adults: Usually 51 to 117 cm (20 to 46 in); the record is 58 in.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Arizona, southern California, Nevada, Utah, southern New Mexico and south-western Texas, and northern Mexico including Baja California.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Dry deserts.[1]

Diet

Lizards, snakes, reptile eggs and small rodents.[1]

Reproduction

Lays 4 to 10 eggs in spring or early summer; they hatch in August or September.[1]

Who described it

First described by Cope in 1867. The brackets around the authority, (Cope, 1867), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

hexalepis: ‘six scales’, from Greek roots: hexa- ‘six’ + -lepis ‘scales’

Western Patchnose Snake: ‘Western’ – where it lives (in the west); ‘Patchnose Snake’ – it has a large patch-like scale on its snout.

Worked out from the Latin and Greek roots and the words in the name; parts SnakeList can’t explain are left out.
