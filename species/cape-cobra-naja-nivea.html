Cape Cobra

Naja nivea (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Cape Cobra is a highly venomous cobra of southern Africa, living in dry savanna, fynbos, bushveld and desert.[1]

Behaviour

Active by day, rarely at night. It lives on the ground but climbs trees and bushes well, for example to raid nests. Birds of prey and honey badgers hunt it.[1]

Diet

Eats almost anything it can catch: other snakes, rodents, lizards, birds - and even carrion.[1]

Reproduction

Lays eggs.[2]

Who described it

First described by Linnaeus in 1758. The brackets around the authority, (Linnaeus, 1758), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

Naja: from the Sanskrit nāga, meaning snake[3]

nivea: ‘snowy white’

Cape Cobra: ‘Cobra’ – from the Portuguese cobra de capelo, ‘hooded snake’.

Worked out from the Latin and Greek roots and the words in the name; parts SnakeList can’t explain are left out.
