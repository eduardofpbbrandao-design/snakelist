European Adder

Vipera berus (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Adder, or Common European Viper, is a small, stocky viper with one of the largest ranges of any snake, from western Europe across to eastern Asia. It lives north of the Arctic Circle in Scandinavia, where no other snake is found. It is Britain's only native venomous snake, but its bite is rarely dangerous to people.[1,2,3]

Identification

Appearance

A dark zigzag stripe runs down the middle of the back, and there is usually a V- or X-shaped mark on top of the head. Completely black adders are common in some areas.[5]

Size

Adults: A small snake: one source gives a maximum length of about 55 cm (21.7 in).[2]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Most of western Europe and on across northern Eurasia into eastern Asia. In Sweden it has been studied about 150 km north of the Arctic Circle.[1,3]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Heathland, grassland and woodland.[4]

Behaviour

Hibernates through the winter.[3,6]

Bites: Its bite is rarely fatal; serious effects are uncommon apart from rare allergic reactions.[2]

Diet

Small mammals, amphibians, birds and reptiles.[4]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young in late summer (August or September), usually about 3 to 20 at a time; in some populations birth comes just before hibernation.[5,6]

Did you know?

  • The adder is the only snake known to live north of the Arctic Circle.[2]

Who described it

First described by Linnaeus in 1758. The brackets around the authority, (Linnaeus, 1758), mean it was first described in a different genus.

What the name means

Vipera: probably from the Latin vivus (alive) and pario (to give birth) - because most vipers bear live young[7]
