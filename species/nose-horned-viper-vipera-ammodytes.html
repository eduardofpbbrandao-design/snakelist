Nose-horned Viper

Vipera ammodytes (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Nose-horned Viper is a venomous viper of south-eastern Europe and western Asia, with a soft 'horn' on the tip of its nose. It is thought to be the most dangerous European viper, with long fangs (up to 13 mm) and strong venom.[1]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Dry, rocky hillsides with few plants; sometimes the edges and clearings of woods. Despite its other name, 'sand viper', it does not live in sandy places.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young.[2]

Who described it

First described by Linnaeus in 1758. The brackets around the authority, (Linnaeus, 1758), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

Vipera: probably from the Latin vivus (alive) and pario (to give birth) - because most vipers bear live young[3]

ammodytes: ‘sand-burrower’

Nose-horned Viper: ‘Viper’ – from the Latin vipera, ‘bearing live young’ - most vipers give birth instead of laying eggs.
