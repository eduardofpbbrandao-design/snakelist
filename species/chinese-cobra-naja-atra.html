Chinese Cobra

Naja atra Cantor, 1842

The Chinese (Taiwan) Cobra is one of the commonest venomous snakes in southern China and causes many snakebites there.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Spreads a hood with a variable pale mark.[1]

Appearance

A hood mark that can look like spectacles, a mask, a horseshoe or an O, and a clearly pale throat.[1]

Size

Adults: Usually 1.2 to 1.5 m, rarely up to 2 m.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: South-eastern China south of the Yangtze (including Hong Kong and Hainan), Taiwan, northern Laos and northern Vietnam.[1,2]

Behaviour

Active by day and at dusk. It usually escapes from people but can be very defensive if cornered.[1]

Reproduction

Lays 6 to 23 eggs between May and July.[1]

Who described it

First described by Cantor in 1842.

Where the name comes from

Naja: from the Sanskrit nāga, meaning snake[3]

atra: ‘black’

Chinese Cobra: ‘Chinese’ – it lives in China; ‘Cobra’ – from the Portuguese cobra de capelo, ‘hooded snake’.

Worked out from the Latin and Greek roots and the words in the name; parts SnakeList can’t explain are left out.
