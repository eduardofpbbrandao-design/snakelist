Lesser African Python

Python natalensis Smith, 1840

The Southern African Rock Python is one of the largest snakes in the world. It is non-venomous and kills prey by squeezing.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Huge, heavy python with a blotched pattern.[1]

Size

Adults: Usually 2.8 to 4 m; over 4.6 m is rare. Females are much bigger than males.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Southern Africa: from Angola, Zambia, Tanzania and Kenya south to South Africa.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Savanna and woodland.[1]

Diet

Mainly small antelopes, jackals, hares and monkeys, occasionally fish and even crocodiles.[1]

Reproduction

Lays 30 to 60 eggs, sometimes over 100. The mother guards and warms them and may stay with the young for two weeks or more after hatching.[1]

Did you know?

  • Mother rock pythons may stay with their babies for weeks after they hatch - unusual for a snake.[1]

Where the name comes from

Python: named after Python, the serpent that Apollo killed at Delphi in Greek myth[3]

natalensis: ‘from Natal (South Africa)’ (the ending -ensis means ‘from’ a place)

Lesser African Python: ‘African’ – it lives in Africa; ‘Python’ – after Python, the giant serpent of Greek myth.
