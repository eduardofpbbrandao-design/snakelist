Common Mock Viper

Psammodynastes pulverulentus (Boie, 1827)

The Common Mock Viper is a small Asian snake that coils and strikes like a viper when threatened, although it is harmless to people.[1]

Who described it

First described by Boie in 1827. The brackets around the authority, (Boie, 1827), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

pulverulentus: ‘dusty’

Common Mock Viper: ‘Common’ – it is the commonest or best-known of its kind; ‘Viper’ – from the Latin vipera, ‘bearing live young’ - most vipers give birth instead of laying eggs.
