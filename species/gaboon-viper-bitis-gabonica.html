Gaboon Viper

Bitis gabonica (Duméril, Bibron & Duméril, 1854)

The Gaboon Viper is a massive, beautifully camouflaged viper of African rainforests. It holds the record for the longest fangs of any snake and is often called the heaviest viper. It spends most of its time lying still on the forest floor, waiting for prey to come close.[1,2]

Identification

Appearance

A very thick-bodied viper whose bold colour pattern gives it excellent camouflage among dead leaves on the forest floor. A pair of small horn-like scales sits between the raised nostrils.[2]

Size

Typical adults: Adults usually measure 125 to 155 cm in total length.[2]

Records: A 1.8 m specimen weighed 11.3 kg with an empty stomach. A 205 cm snake from Sierra Leone is often quoted as the record, but snakes from that region are now treated as the West African Gaboon Viper. Heavier weights are unverified.[2]

Fangs: Its fangs are the longest of any snake: the record is about 5 cm.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Sub-Saharan Africa, mainly in and around its rainforests. West African snakes, once a subspecies of this species, are now listed separately as Rhinoceros Viper.[2,3]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Rainforest and the woodland around it, mostly at low altitudes, where it lies hidden in leaf litter.[2]

Behaviour

An ambush hunter: its camouflage is so good that prey walks right past it, and then it lunges and bites.[2,4]

Diet

Mainly small mammals such as rodents, plus ground-living birds (francolins, doves) and frogs and toads.[3]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young after a pregnancy of about seven months, often 30 to 40 babies at once.[4]

Did you know?

  • Its fangs, up to about 5 cm long, are the longest of any snake in the world.[1]

Who described it

First described by Duméril, Bibron & Duméril in 1854. The brackets around the authority, (Duméril, Bibron & Duméril, 1854), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

gabonica: ‘of Gabon’ – named after where it lives

Gaboon Viper: ‘Viper’ – from the Latin vipera, ‘bearing live young’ - most vipers give birth instead of laying eggs.
