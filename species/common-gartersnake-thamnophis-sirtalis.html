Common Gartersnake

Thamnophis sirtalis (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Common Gartersnake is one of North America's most widespread snakes, found across most of the non-desert parts of the continent, and it reaches farther north than any other North American snake. It is not dangerous to people and copes with cold better than most snakes.[1,2]

Identification

Appearance

Most have yellowish stripes along a black, brown or green body, but the colours vary a great deal - including green, blue, gold, red and orange forms.[1,4]

Size

Adults: In South Dakota adults measure about 46 to 76 cm (18 to 30 in). Few grow longer than about 1.2 m; the maximum given is about 137 cm.[2,3]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Much of North America, from the south of Canada through most of the non-desert United States.[1,2]

Behaviour

Mostly active by day. It handles colder temperatures than many snakes and may even bask on rocks on mild winter days.[1]

Winter: Gartersnakes hibernate in large groups and mate in spring as they come out of their winter dens. In Manitoba, Canada, the huge spring gatherings of the Red-sided Gartersnake at Narcisse draw visitors from around the world.[1,5]

Diet

Mainly frogs, toads and salamanders, but a wide range of other prey has been recorded, from earthworms and fish to voles.[2]

Did you know?

  • No other North American snake lives as far north as the Common Gartersnake.[2]
