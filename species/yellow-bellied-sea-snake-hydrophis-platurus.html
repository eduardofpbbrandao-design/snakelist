Yellow-bellied Sea Snake

Hydrophis platurus (Linnaeus, 1766)

The Yellow-bellied Sea Snake spends its whole life in the open ocean and never needs to come ashore. It is one of the most widespread snakes on Earth, drifting across the tropical Indian and Pacific Oceans with the currents. It is venomous, but because it lives far out at sea, bites to people are rare.[1,2]

Identification

Appearance

Unmistakable: black or dark brown on the back and bright yellow underneath, with a sharp line between the two colours. The tail is flattened from side to side like a paddle and is yellow with black spots or bars. The head is long and narrow.[1,5]

Size

Adults: Reaches up to about 80 cm.[3]

Range

Published distribution

Native: Tropical and warm waters of the Indian and Pacific Oceans, from the coasts of Africa across to the Pacific coast of the Americas, from southern California to northern Peru. It is the only sea snake to have reached Hawaii, and the only one found - rarely - in the Atlantic. Storms and warm currents sometimes carry it far beyond its usual range.[1]

Ecology

Habitat

Completely pelagic (living in the open ocean). It is often found along slicks and drift lines - the lines where surface currents meet and floating debris collects - and it is the only air-breathing marine predator known to hunt at these surface features.[1]

Behaviour

It lets surface currents and storms carry it around the ocean. It is said to be able to swim backwards as well as forwards.[1,2]

Drinking: Like other sea snakes it needs fresh water: it drinks the layer of rainwater that forms on the sea surface after rain.[1]

Diet

Small fish, including the young of larger species. It floats still near the surface and catches fish that gather beneath it for shelter.[4,2]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young in the water instead of laying eggs; litters of about 3-8 babies are reported.[1]

Conservation

LCListed as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List; no overall population estimate exists.[2,1]
