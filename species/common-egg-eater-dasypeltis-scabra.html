Common Egg Eater

Dasypeltis scabra (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Common (Rhombic) Egg Eater is a harmless African snake that eats nothing but eggs. It has almost no teeth.[1]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Most habitats from sea level to 2,600 m, except closed forest and true desert.[1]

Behaviour

Active at night. Mostly on the ground, but it climbs rocks and trees to raid birds' nests.[1]

Diet

Only eggs. Ridges inside its mouth, like fingerprints, help it grip the shell.[1]

Reproduction

Lays eggs.[2]

Who described it

First described by Linnaeus in 1758. The brackets around the authority, (Linnaeus, 1758), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

scabra: ‘rough’

Common Egg Eater: ‘Common’ – it is the commonest or best-known of its kind; ‘Egg Eater’ – it eats birds' eggs.
