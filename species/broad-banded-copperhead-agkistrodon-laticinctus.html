Broad-banded Copperhead

Agkistrodon laticinctus Gloyd & Conant, 1934

The Broad-banded Copperhead is a venomous pit viper of the south-central United States. It used to be counted as a subspecies of the Eastern Copperhead.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Wide dark crossbands on a tan body.[1]

Appearance

Light tan with wide dark brown crossbands (the 'broad bands'); the colour varies from red-brown to grey-brown.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Kansas, Oklahoma and central Texas, and the edges of Chihuahua and Coahuila in Mexico.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Light woodland with plenty of ground debris, near permanent water; it avoids crowded areas.[1]

Behaviour

Secretive and active at night.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young. Females can sometimes reproduce without a male.[2]

Where the name comes from

Agkistrodon: from the Greek ankistron (fishhook) and odon (tooth), probably for the curved fangs[3]

laticinctus: ‘broad-banded’, from Latin roots: lati- ‘broad’ + -cinctus ‘banded’

Broad-banded Copperhead: ‘Broad-banded’ – for its broad bands; ‘Copperhead’ – its head is copper-coloured.
