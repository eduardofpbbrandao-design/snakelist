Montpellier Snake

Malpolon monspessulanus (Hermann, 1804)

The Montpellier Snake is a large, fast, day-active snake of the western Mediterranean, very common in Spain and Portugal. It is mildly venomous with fangs at the back of the mouth, but only a few cases of people being envenomed are known.[1]

Identification

Size

Adults: Sources usually give a maximum of about 2 m; some give up to 2.55 m and 2 kg. Often described as Spain's longest snake.[1,3]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: The Iberian Peninsula, France and Liguria in north-western Italy, and north-western Africa (Morocco and north-western Algeria). Snakes further east, from the Balkans and Anatolia to the Middle East, are now treated as a separate species, Malpolon insignitus; where the two meet in eastern Morocco and Algeria is still debated.[2,4]

Behaviour

Active during the day. Like its relatives the sand snakes, it has been seen rubbing its body - both young and adults do this - a habit that may play a part in social life and breeding.[1,2]

Diet

Mainly lizards; rodents, other snakes and small birds are also eaten.[1,2]

Classification notes

It belongs to the family Psammophiidae (sand snakes and allies).[2]

It was first described in 1804 as Coluber monspessulanus, in a work by Johann Hermann published after his death; the name refers to Montpellier.[5]
