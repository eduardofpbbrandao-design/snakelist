Australian Black Snake

Pseudechis porphyriacus (Shaw, 1794)

The Red-bellied Black Snake is a venomous snake of eastern Australia and one of the snakes people there meet most often. It is not aggressive and usually moves away from people.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Shiny black back with red or orange sides.[1]

Appearance

Glossy black on top with bright red or orange sides fading to a pink or dull red belly; light grey snout and a black tail.[1]

Size

Adults: About 1.25 m long on average.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Eastern Australia: Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria and South Australia - including the areas around Brisbane, Sydney, Canberra, Melbourne and Adelaide.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Woodland, bushland, plains and urban forest, often near water.[1]

Behaviour

Hides in logs, old burrows and grass tussocks, and can escape into water - one stayed under for 23 minutes. In spring males wrestle each other, trying to push the other's head down.[1]

Diet

Mostly frogs, but also reptiles and other prey.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young.[2]

Did you know?

  • It can float still on the water so that it looks just like a stick.[1]
