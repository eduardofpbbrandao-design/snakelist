Amazon Tree Boa

Corallus hortulana (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Amazon Tree Boa is a slim, non-venomous boa of South American forests. It comes in a huge range of colours and spends most of its life in trees, hunting at night.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Thin body with a big head and a long tail for gripping branches.[1]
  • Five dark stripes running back from the eyes (usually).[1]

Appearance

Colours range from black, brown or grey to red, orange or yellow, plain, speckled or banded. There are usually five dark stripes running back from the eyes, and the eyes shine at night.[1]

Size

Adults: Usually about 1.5 to 2 m long.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Amazonia and the Guianas to south-eastern Brazil: Colombia (east of the Andes), Venezuela, Guyana, Suriname, French Guiana, Brazil, Ecuador, Peru and Bolivia.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Most common in humid rainforest, but also found in drier savannas and dry forests. Usually found well above the ground in trees, though it sometimes feeds and breeds on the ground.[1]

Behaviour

Mainly active at night and lives in trees.[1]

Diet

Like all boas it kills by squeezing (constriction). It eats a very wide range of animals: tree frogs, lizards, birds, bats, rodents and other small mammals. Its long teeth help it hold on to prey in the trees.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young.[1,2]

Did you know?

  • Two snakes of the very same species can look completely different - one bright yellow, another dark brown.[1]
