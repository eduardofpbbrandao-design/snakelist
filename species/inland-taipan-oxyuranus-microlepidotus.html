Inland Taipan

Oxyuranus microlepidotus (McCoy, 1879)

The Inland Taipan has the most toxic venom of any land snake in laboratory tests on mice. Yet it is far from the most dangerous snake to people: it is shy, prefers to escape, and lives in remote, dry inland Australia, so bites are very rare.[1,2]

Identification

Appearance

A medium-large elapid whose colour changes with the seasons: darker in winter and paler in summer. The darker winter colour probably helps it soak up more of the sun's heat when it is cold.[1,3]

Size

Adults: Sources give lengths of around 1.8 to 2.5 m, with about 2 m typical.[2,3]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Found only in Australia: south-western Queensland and north-eastern and central South Australia.[1,3]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Remote semi-arid country in the interior of Australia.[2]

Behaviour

Very shy: it nearly always tries to flee rather than defend itself.[2,3]

Diet

Mostly small mammals. Its favourite prey is the Long-haired Rat; it also eats house mice and small marsupials called dasyurids.[1]

Reproduction

Lays eggs; one source reports clutches of 12 to 20 eggs that hatch after about 66 days.[3]

Did you know?

  • In tests on mice, the venom from one bite would be enough to kill around 250,000 mice.[4]
  • It turns darker in winter, like putting on a heat-absorbing coat.[1]

Who described it

First described by McCoy in 1879. The brackets around the authority, (McCoy, 1879), mean it was first described in a different genus.

What the name means

Oxyuranus: from the Greek oxys (sharp, needle-like) and ouranos (arch, vault of the sky) - for a needle-like point on the arch of the palate[5]
