Lataste's Viper

Vipera latastei

Lataste's Viper, also called the Snub-nosed Viper, is a small viper of the Iberian Peninsula and north-western Africa. Its populations are fragmented across Portugal and Spain.[1,2]

Identification

Appearance

Its most remarkable feature is the upturned tip of the snout, forming a small "nose horn" - the origin of its other name, Snub-nosed Viper.[1,4]

Size

Adults: Normally less than 60 cm; the maximum recorded total length is about 72 cm.[1,2]

Similar species

  • Viperine Snake Natrix maura – a harmless water snake that copies viper patterns and behaviour; it lacks the upturned snout and is usually found in or near water.[5]

Range

Published distribution

Native: The Iberian Peninsula (Portugal and Spain) and north-western North Africa, including the Rif mountains of Morocco; it may now be extinct in Tunisia. The southern subspecies V. l. gaditana lives in the south of Portugal and Spain.[1]

Ecology

Habitat

Rocky and scrubby country, including stone walls and sometimes coastal dunes, where its colours blend in well.[1,4]

Conservation

VUListed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List according to Wikipedia; some other sources give Near Threatened, so check the current Red List entry.[1]
