Asp Viper

Vipera aspis (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Asp Viper is a venomous viper of south-western Europe. Its bite is very painful and can be more serious than an Adder's, so it needs hospital care. In Italy it causes most snakebites.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Snout tip turned up slightly.[1]
  • Wide triangular head and a short tail.[1]

Appearance

A broad, triangular head clearly wider than the neck, and a snout tip that turns up slightly. The tail is very short.[1]

Size

Adults: About 60 to 65 cm on average; males reach up to 85 cm, females rarely more than 75 cm.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: France, Andorra, north-eastern Spain, the far south-west of Germany (southern Black Forest), Switzerland, Italy and north-western Slovenia.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Warm, sunny places with some plant cover and fairly dry soil - sunny slopes and scrub in hills and low mountains, often on limestone.[1]

Behaviour

Bites: Its bite is very painful; in old studies about 4% of untreated bites were fatal. Anyone bitten needs medical help quickly.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young.[2]

Who described it

First described by Linnaeus in 1758. The brackets around the authority, (Linnaeus, 1758), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

Vipera: probably from the Latin vivus (alive) and pario (to give birth) - because most vipers bear live young[3]

aspis: ‘asp, small viper’

Asp Viper: ‘Asp’ – from the Greek aspis, an old name for a venomous snake; ‘Viper’ – from the Latin vipera, ‘bearing live young’ - most vipers give birth instead of laying eggs.
