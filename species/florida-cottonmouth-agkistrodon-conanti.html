Florida Cottonmouth

Agkistrodon conanti Gloyd, 1969

The Florida Cottonmouth is a venomous pit viper of Florida and southern Georgia, found in almost every kind of wetland there, even brackish water and islands.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Thick, dark pit viper near water.[1]
  • Two dark vertical lines on the snout.[1]

Appearance

Its face markings are usually lighter and cleaner than the northern cottonmouth's, with two dark vertical lines on the snout.[1]

Size

Adults: Up to 1,892 mm (74.5 in).[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: The Florida peninsula, including many barrier islands and keys, and southern Georgia.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Swamps, edges of cypress ponds and wooded water margins - but it is not entirely tied to water.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to 1 to 15 live young, mostly in summer; females usually breed every other year. Females can sometimes reproduce without a male.[1,2]

Where the name comes from

Agkistrodon: from the Greek ankistron (fishhook) and odon (tooth), probably for the curved fangs[3]

conanti: named after a person called Conant

Florida Cottonmouth: ‘Florida’ – after Florida.
