Copperhead

Agkistrodon contortrix

The Copperhead is a medium-sized pit viper of the eastern United States, named for its coppery head. Its pattern of hourglass-shaped bands makes it hard to spot among dead leaves. It is venomous, but it usually bites only if someone steps on it, grabs it or comes very close.[1,2]

Identification

Appearance

A tan to pinkish snake with chestnut-brown or mahogany crossbands. Seen from above the bands are narrow in the middle of the back and wide on the sides, like an hourglass; sometimes they are broken in the middle.[1,5]

Size

Adults: Usually about 61 to 91 cm (24 to 36 in) long; northern adults are often 66 to 86 cm.[1,2]

Variation

Young: Babies have a bright yellow tail tip, which they wiggle like a worm or caterpillar to lure frogs and lizards within striking range.[4]

Ecology

Habitat

In many areas it lives in high, dry, rocky, well-wooded places dominated by oaks and hickories.[5]

Diet

Frogs, toads, lizards, insects and caterpillars, mice and birds.[1,2]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young in late summer or autumn. Litters range from 2 to 17, usually 6 to 9; in Indiana, females give birth to 4 to 8 young every other year.[1,5]

Taxonomy

Family
Viperidae (vipers and pit vipers)
Genus
Agkistrodon – from the Greek ankistron (fishhook) and odon (tooth), probably for the curved fangs[3]
Species
Agkistrodon contortrix

A 2015 genetic study proposed splitting the copperhead into two species: the Eastern Copperhead (A. contortrix) and the Broad-banded Copperhead (A. laticinctus) of the south-central United States.[4,1]
