Rough Greensnake

Opheodrys aestivus (Linnaeus, 1766)

The Rough Greensnake is a slender, bright green, non-venomous snake of the south-eastern and central United States. It lives in shrubs and low trees, often above water, and feeds almost entirely on insects and spiders.[1,2]

Identification

Appearance

Very thin, with a long tail, plain bright green above and whitish or yellowish below. Its back scales are keeled (they have a ridge down the middle), which gives the "rough" in its name and separates it from the Smooth Greensnake.[2,3]

Size

Adults: Most adults are about 35-82 cm (14-33 in) in total length; the record is 116 cm. Some sources give ranges up to about 114 cm (45 in) for large adults.[2,3]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: The south-eastern and south-central United States, from New Jersey to Florida and west to Kansas, Oklahoma and Texas, with records extending into north-eastern Mexico.[1]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Dense vegetation - shrubs, vines and low trees - especially along the edges of streams, lakes and wetlands, where it often rests in branches overhanging water.[1,3]

Behaviour

Largely active by day and an excellent climber. Its green colour and habit of staying still among leaves make it hard to spot.[1,2]

Diet

Mostly insects and spiders: crickets, grasshoppers and caterpillars make up much of what it eats. Snails and small tree frogs are taken occasionally.[1,2]

Reproduction

Females lay eggs, usually about 3-12 per clutch (sources range from 1 to 14). Several females sometimes share a nest site, and up to 75 eggs have been found together in one such communal nest.[1,2]

Who described it

First described by Linnaeus in 1766. The brackets around the authority, (Linnaeus, 1766), mean it was first described in a different genus.

What the name means

Opheodrys: from the Greek ophis (snake) and drys (tree) - a tree snake[4]
