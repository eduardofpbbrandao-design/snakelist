Boomslang

Dispholidus typus (Smith, 1828)

The Boomslang is a slender, highly venomous tree snake of sub-Saharan Africa. Unlike most of its family, the colubrids, its bite can kill a person. Its venom acts slowly: symptoms may not appear until many hours after a bite.[1]

Identification

Appearance

A slim snake with very large eyes with round pupils, giving it sharp eyesight. Colour varies from plain brown to green; males are usually more colourful than females.[1,3]

Variation

Sexes: Males tend to be brighter; female hatchlings are pale brown.[1,3]

Behaviour

An active hunter in trees and bushes that relies on its excellent vision. Its fangs are at the back of the mouth (it is "rear-fanged").[1]

Diet

Lizards, birds, bird eggs and frogs, which it swallows whole after biting.[2,4]

Reproduction

Lays eggs.[2]

Did you know?

  • In 1957 the famous American herpetologist Karl Patterson Schmidt died after being bitten by a young boomslang.[1,2]
