Bullsnake

Pituophis catenifer (Blainville, 1835)

The Gopher Snake (its eastern form is the Bullsnake) is a big, non-venomous snake of western and central North America. It is often mistaken for a rattlesnake, which it imitates when it is scared.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Big blotched snake without a rattle.[1]
  • Hisses loudly and copies a rattlesnake when scared.[1]

Appearance

Yellowish or sandy brown with a row of large dark brown or black blotches and smaller dark spots on the sides; the belly is yellowish. It has no rattle.[1]

Size

Adults: Usually about 91 to 213 cm (3 to 7 ft) long.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Western and central North America, from southern British Columbia, Alberta and Saskatchewan through the western and central United States to northern Mexico and Baja California.[1,2]

Behaviour

When threatened it puffs up, hisses, coils like a rattlesnake and shakes its tail; it often strikes with its mouth closed, just bumping with its nose to warn the attacker.[1]

Reproduction

Lays eggs.[2]

Did you know?

  • Gopher snakes on islands with no rattlesnakes shake their tails for less time than those living alongside rattlesnakes.[1]
