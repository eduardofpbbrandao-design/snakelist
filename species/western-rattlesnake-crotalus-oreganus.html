Western Rattlesnake

Crotalus oreganus Holbrook, 1840

The Western (Northern Pacific) Rattlesnake is a venomous pit viper of western North America, from southern British Columbia to Baja California.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Rattle on the tail tip.[1]
  • Heat-sensing pit between eye and nostril.[1]

Size

Adults: Size varies a lot between populations; mainland snakes often reach about 100 cm, and the record is 162.6 cm.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Southern interior British Columbia in Canada, much of the western USA, and the Baja California Peninsula of Mexico.[1,2]

Diet

Finds prey with the heat-sensing pits on its face: birds, eggs, small mammals from mice to rabbits, and small reptiles and amphibians; young ones eat insects. Prey that runs off after the bite is tracked down once the venom works.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young.[2]

Where the name comes from

Crotalus: from the Greek krotalon, a rattle or castanet - after the rattle at the tip of the tail[3]

Western Rattlesnake: ‘Western’ – where it lives (in the west); ‘Rattlesnake’ – it shakes the rattle at the tip of its tail.
