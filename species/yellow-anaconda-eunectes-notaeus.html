Yellow Anaconda

Eunectes notaeus Cope, 1862

The Yellow Anaconda is a big, non-venomous water boa of southern South America. It is one of the largest snakes in the world, though smaller than its relative the Green Anaconda. Like all boas, it kills its prey by squeezing.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Yellowish body with black blotches.[1]
  • Usually in or near water.[1]

Appearance

Yellow, golden-tan or greenish-yellow with black or dark brown saddles, blotches and spots.[1]

Size

Adults: Usually weigh 25 to 35 kg, and some over 55 kg; females are generally bigger than males.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: The Paraguay River and its tributaries, from the Pantanal of Bolivia, Paraguay and western Brazil to north-eastern Argentina and northern Uruguay.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Mostly in water: swamps, marshes and the bushy banks of slow rivers and streams.[1]

Behaviour

Lives alone except in the mating season and spends most of its time in the water, coming onto land to mate, move to other water or hunt.[1]

Diet

Hunts mainly in shallow water, both by waiting in ambush and by searching. Its prey is almost all water or wetland animals - wading birds are taken most when floods go down, and apple snails and fish are eaten too.[1]

Reproduction

Mates in April and May; several males may wrap around one female in a 'breeding ball'. About 6 months later the female gives birth to live young.[1]

Did you know?

  • Several male anacondas can wrap around one female at once - a tangle called a breeding ball.[1]

Where the name comes from

Eunectes: Greek for "good swimmer" - fitting for the anacondas[3]

Yellow Anaconda: ‘Yellow’ – its colour.
