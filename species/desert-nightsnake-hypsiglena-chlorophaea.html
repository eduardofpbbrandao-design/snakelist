Desert Nightsnake

Hypsiglena chlorophaea Cope, 1860

The Desert Nightsnake is a small, secretive, night-active snake of western North America. Its mild venom is no danger to people.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Small, pale and blotched, out at night.[1]

Appearance

Small and slender; beige, yellowish or grey with paired dark blotches down the back.[1]

Size

Adults: About 30 to 66 cm long; females are bigger.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: The western and south-western USA, north-western Mexico, and a small isolated population in south-central British Columbia, Canada.[1,2]

Behaviour

Active at night and dusk; an ambush hunter that uses its venom to subdue prey.[1]

Diet

Mostly small lizards and lizard eggs, plus small snakes, frogs and salamanders.[1]

Reproduction

Mates in spring and lays 2 to 9 eggs (usually about 4) between April and July.[1]
