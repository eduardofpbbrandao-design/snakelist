Timber Rattlesnake

Crotalus horridus Linnaeus, 1758

The Timber Rattlesnake is a large pit viper found only in the eastern United States, from about New York south to northern Florida. In many areas its life revolves around traditional winter dens shared with other rattlesnakes.[1,2,3]

Identification

Appearance

Yellowish, grey, brown or black above, with darker blotches or crossbands, and a rattle on the tail.[4]

Variation

Regional: Snakes from cooler places - farther north or higher up - are often darker.[4]

Size

Adults: Roughly 76 to 152 cm (2.5 to 5 ft); Maine gives about 91 to 132 cm.[2,4]

Distribution

Native: The eastern United States, from about New York to northern Florida.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Virginia (regional): In western Virginia it lives in upland hardwood and mixed oak-pine forest with rocky ledges or talus slopes.[3]

Behaviour

Dens: In parts of Virginia, its life centres on communal dens used for generations (hibernacula) and on sites where females give birth (rookeries).[3]

What the name means
