Smooth Green Snake

Opheodrys vernalis (Harlan, 1827)

The Smooth Green Snake is a small, gentle, non-venomous snake of Canada and the northern United States. It is bright green on top, which hides it perfectly in grass.[1]

Identification

Appearance

Plain light green on the back with a yellow or white belly. Its back scales are smooth - the Rough Green Snake looks similar but has ridged (keeled) scales.[1]

Size

Adults: A slender snake; the longest one measured was 66 cm. The tail is a quarter to half of its length, longer in males.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Southern Canada and scattered parts of the northern and central United States, with small isolated populations further south and west, and in Chihuahua, Mexico.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Marshes, meadows, open woods and the edges of streams.[1]

Behaviour

Hawks, herons, bears, raccoons, foxes and pet cats all hunt it. People sometimes collect it as a pet, but it does not survive well in captivity.[1]

Reproduction

Usually lays eggs, but females keep them inside their bodies for a long time first - up to about a month - so the young are already well grown when the eggs are laid.[2]

Who described it

First described by Harlan in 1827. The brackets around the authority, (Harlan, 1827), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

Opheodrys: from the Greek ophis (snake) and drys (tree) - a tree snake[3]

vernalis: ‘of spring’

Smooth Green Snake: ‘Smooth’ – its smooth scales; ‘Green’ – its colour.
