Eastern Coral Snake

Micrurus fulvius (Linnaeus, 1766)

The Eastern Coral Snake is a slender, brightly ringed elapid of the south-eastern United States. Its venom acts on the nervous system and is dangerous, but it is shy and secretive, and bites are very rare.[1,2]

Identification

Appearance

Ringed with red, yellow and black around the whole body. In this species the red rings touch the yellow ones; a well-known rhyme ("red on yellow can kill a fellow") is based on that. Still, never rely on a rhyme to decide whether a snake is safe.[1,3]

Size

Adults: Usually less than 80 cm (31 in) in total length.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: The south-eastern United States, including Florida - one of its other names is Florida Coral Snake.[1,3]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Woodlands, pine and scrub forests, marshes and areas near water.[3]

Behaviour

Secretive and shy; it usually avoids people.[2,3]

Reproduction

Lays about three to 12 eggs in June, which hatch in September.[3]
