Reticulated Python

Malayopython reticulatus (Schneider, 1801)

The Reticulated Python is the longest snake in the world: a non-venomous constrictor of the forests and rivers of Southeast Asia. Its name comes from the net-like (reticulated) pattern on its back. It swims very well, which helped it reach many small islands across its range.[1,2]

Identification

Appearance

A long, fairly slender python with a complex, net-like (reticulated) pattern along the back - the feature that gives it its name.[1]

Size

Typical adults: Most adults measure a few metres; zoo sources give a range of about 1.5 to 6.5 m, with 3 to 6 m common.[2,3]

Largest records: The longest scientifically verified specimen measured 6.95 m (22.8 ft) and weighed 59 kg. Reports of snakes up to 10 m exist but are not verified, and sources disagree about them.[1,2]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Southeast Asia, from Bangladesh and Myanmar through Indochina and the Malay Peninsula to Indonesia and the Philippines, and on islands west of New Guinea.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Tropical forest, usually near rivers and other water.[1,2]

Diet

Young pythons eat small prey such as rats; as they grow they take larger mammals - up to deer and pigs - and also birds.[1,3]

Reproduction

Lays eggs - usually 20 to 50, though clutches of more than 100 have been recorded. The female coils around the eggs to keep them warm. Well-fed females may breed every one to three years.[1,2]

Did you know?

  • It is the world's longest snake - the record verified specimen was nearly 7 m long.[2]

Conservation

LC

Least Concern on the IUCN Red List[1,2,3]

Global assessment · assessed 2018

Listed as Least Concern by Britannica and zoo sources citing the IUCN; one source says it has not been assessed, so treat this with some care.

Still widespread, but large numbers are collected for their skins, for meat and for the pet trade, and big pythons are often killed out of fear.[1,2]

Who described it

First described by Schneider in 1801. The brackets around the authority, (Schneider, 1801), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Classification notes

Long called Python reticulatus. Genetic studies published in 2014 moved it, with the Timor Python, into a new genus, Malayopython.[2]
