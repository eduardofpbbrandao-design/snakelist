Red-lipped Snake

Crotaphopeltis hotamboeia (Laurenti, 1768)

The Red-lipped (Herald) Snake is a common, mildly venomous snake of Africa south of the Sahara. It is harmless to people.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Red (or yellow) upper lip and a dark head.[1]

Appearance

Olive-green or grey body with white speckles, a black head, and a red, yellow, white or black upper lip.[1]

Size

Adults: About 70 cm long on average, up to 1 m.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Widespread in Africa south of the Sahara, from Senegal and Ethiopia to South Africa.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Marshy areas in lowland forest, moist savanna, grassland and fynbos.[1]

Diet

Amphibians, including rain frogs, and lizards.[1]

Reproduction

Lays 6 to 19 eggs in early summer.[1]

Where the name comes from

Red-lipped Snake: ‘Red-lipped’ – for its red lips.
