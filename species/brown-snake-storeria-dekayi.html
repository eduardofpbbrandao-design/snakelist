Brown Snake

Storeria dekayi (Holbrook, 1839)

De Kay's Brown Snake is a small, harmless snake of eastern North America and Central America. It is very common and is often found in towns, gardens and city parks.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Small brown snake with a pale stripe down the back.[1]
  • Two rows of small black spots along the stripe.[1]

Appearance

Brown to grey with a paler stripe down the middle of the back, edged with small black spots. The belly is pale brown or pinkish with tiny black dots at the edges.[1]

Size

Adults: Usually less than 30 cm (12 in) long; the record is about 49 cm. Newborns are only about 7.7 cm long.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Southern Ontario and Quebec, most of the eastern half of the United States, and south through Mexico to Guatemala and Honduras.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Most kinds of wet and dry habitats east of the Great Plains, from sea level to about 1,400 m. It likes moist places and is most often found near people - in cities more than in the countryside.[1]

Diet

Mainly slugs, snails and earthworms.[1]

Reproduction

Mates in spring after its winter rest and gives birth to live young. Females can store sperm, so the babies are born when food is plentiful.[1]
