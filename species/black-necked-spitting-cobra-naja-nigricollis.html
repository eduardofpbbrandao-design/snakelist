Black-necked Spitting Cobra

Naja nigricollis Reinhardt, 1843

The Black-necked Spitting Cobra is a common venomous cobra of Africa south of the Sahara. When threatened it can spray venom from its fangs towards an attacker's eyes. Its bite needs medical care, although few untreated bites are fatal.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Spreads a hood when threatened.[1]

Appearance

Colour varies with region. Many are black or grey with a broad black band under the neck, often with a pink or orange bar; others are yellowish-brown or coppery without the neck band.[1]

Size

Adults: Usually about 1.2 to 2.2 m long.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Widespread in western, central and eastern Africa and parts of southern Africa, from Senegal and Mauritania east to Ethiopia and Somalia and south to Angola and Zambia; absent from the Congo Basin rainforest.[1,2]

Behaviour

Can be active by day or by night, depending on the season, place and temperature. Snake eagles and other birds of prey hunt it.[1]

Spitting: When threatened it can squirt venom from its fangs. Venom in the eyes is very painful and needs to be washed out quickly.[1]

Diet

Mainly small rats and mice, but also birds, fish, lizards, eggs and other snakes.[1]

Reproduction

Lays eggs.[1,2]

Where the name comes from

Naja: from the Sanskrit nāga, meaning snake[3]

nigricollis: ‘black-necked’, from Latin roots: nigri- ‘black’ + -collis ‘necked’

Black-necked Spitting Cobra: ‘Black-necked’ – for its black neck; ‘Spitting Cobra’ – it can spit venom at the eyes of an attacker.
