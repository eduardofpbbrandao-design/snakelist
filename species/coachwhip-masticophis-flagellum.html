Coachwhip

Masticophis flagellum (Shaw, 1802)

The Coachwhip is a long, very fast, non-venomous snake of the southern United States and northern Mexico. Its tail scales look like the braids of a leather whip.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Very long, thin and fast.[1]
  • Big eyes and a small head.[1]

Appearance

Thin body, small head and big eyes with round pupils. Colour varies with the soil it lives on - tan with darker flecks, dark brown to black, or even pink in parts of west Texas.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Across the southern United States from coast to coast, and the northern half of Mexico.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Open, sandy places: pine woods, old fields, prairies, sandhill scrub and coastal dunes.[1]

Behaviour

Active by day, alert and quick to flee - it can move at up to about 6 km/h. It sometimes lifts its head above the grass to look around. If cornered it strikes; the bite hurts but is harmless. Coachwhips roam over some of the largest home ranges known for any snake.[1]

Diet

A fast hunter that eats almost anything it can catch: other snakes, lizards, frogs, rodents, birds and their eggs, and insects such as cicadas.[1]

Reproduction

Lays eggs.[2]

Did you know?

  • Coachwhips sometimes 'periscope' - lifting their heads high above the grass to look around.[1]
