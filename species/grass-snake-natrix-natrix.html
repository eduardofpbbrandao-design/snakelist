Grass Snake

Natrix natrix (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Grass Snake (now often called the Eastern Grass Snake) is a non-venomous snake of Eurasia, usually found near water. In 2017 a DNA study showed that the grass snakes of western Europe are a separate species, the Barred Grass Snake, so the name Natrix natrix now covers the populations from Scandinavia and central Europe eastwards.[1,2]

Published distribution

Native: Much of Scandinavia and central and eastern Europe, east to Central Asia. Grass snakes in Britain belong to the Barred Grass Snake; records of N. natrix there are thought to come from imported animals.[1,3]

Who described it

First described by Linnaeus in 1758. The brackets around the authority, (Linnaeus, 1758), mean it was first described in a different genus.

What the name means

Natrix: the classical Latin word for a water snake, related to the German Natter and English adder[4]

Classification notes

A 2017 study in Scientific Reports, using mitochondrial and nuclear DNA, raised the western grass snakes to a species of their own, Natrix helvetica (Barred Grass Snake), found from about the Rhine west to the Pyrenees and in Britain. The Iberian and North African grass snakes had already been recognised in 2016 as N. astreptophora.[2,3,5]
