Mole Snake

Pseudaspis cana (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Mole Snake is a big, non-venomous snake of southern and eastern Africa. It eats moles and rats, so it helps control pests.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Thick, plain-coloured body with a small pointed head.[1]

Appearance

A firm, tube-shaped body, a small head and a pointed snout. Adults are mostly one colour, from yellow to brown or black.[1]

Size

Adults: Up to about 2 m long.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Most of southern Africa, from Angola and Kenya to South Africa.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Lives in the old burrows of other animals.[1]

Diet

Golden moles, rodents and other small mammals; sometimes seabird eggs and chicks.[1]

Reproduction

Mates in late spring (October) and gives birth to live young.[1]
