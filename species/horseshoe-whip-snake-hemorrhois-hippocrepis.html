Horseshoe Whip Snake

Hemorrhois hippocrepis (Linnaeus, 1758)

Horseshoe Whip Snake (Hemorrhois hippocrepis) is a non-venomous snake of the family Colubridae, found in Algeria, Gibraltar, Morocco, Portugal, Spain and Tunisia. It is invasive in Ibiza, Formentera and Mallorca, where people introduced it. People introduced it to Sardinia and Pantelleria.

Distribution

Introduced populations

Ibiza, Formentera and Mallorca: Invasive. Arrived with imported olive trees (first seen on Ibiza in 2003); on Ibiza it feeds mainly on the endemic Ibiza wall lizard, which is declining fast where the snake has spread.[1]

Sardinia and Pantelleria: Introduced by people. The Italian snakes share their genes with snakes from Tunisia and eastern Algeria, so they were most likely brought from North Africa by people.[2]
