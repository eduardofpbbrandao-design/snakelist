Common Watersnake

Nerodia sipedon (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Common Watersnake (Northern Water Snake) is a large, non-venomous snake of eastern and central North America. It is often mistaken for the venomous Cottonmouth.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Usually in or near water.[1]

Appearance

Brown, grey, reddish or brownish-black with dark crossbands near the head and blotches further back; the pattern can fade as it gets older.[1]

Size

Adults: Up to 135 cm. In one study females averaged 81.4 cm and males 69.6 cm; females are heavier too.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Eastern and central North America, from southern Ontario and Quebec south to the Gulf states, and west to Colorado.[1,2]

Introduced: An introduced population was found in California in 2007.[1]

Behaviour

Active by day and by night and often seen basking on rocks, stumps or branches near water.[1]

Diet

By day it hunts among water plants for small fish, tadpoles, frogs, crayfish, insects, salamanders and more; at night it catches minnows resting in shallow water.[1]

Reproduction

Mates from April to June and gives birth to live young between August and October - about eight on average, but up to 30. The babies are 19 to 23 cm long and look after themselves from birth.[1]
