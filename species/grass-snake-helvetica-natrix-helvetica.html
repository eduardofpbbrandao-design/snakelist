Grass Snake (helvetica)

Natrix helvetica (Lacépède, 1789)

The Barred Grass Snake is a harmless snake of western Europe that usually lives in or near water. It was counted as part of the Grass Snake until 2017, when DNA studies showed it is a separate species. It is the grass snake of Great Britain.[1]

How to recognise it

  • Yellow-and-black collar behind the head.[1]

Appearance

Grey-green with black bars along the sides, a pale belly and a yellow-and-black collar behind the head.[1]

Size

Adults: Can grow to more than a metre long.[1]

Distribution

Native: Great Britain (north to southern Scotland), the Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg, western Germany, France, Switzerland, Austria, Italy and northern Spain.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

A strong swimmer often found near fresh water, especially pond edges, but also open woodland and the edges of fields and woods where it can bask.[1]

Diet

Mainly amphibians, especially common toads and common frogs, plus fish, small mammals and nestling birds. It searches actively for food, often at the water's edge.[1]

Reproduction

Mates in spring soon after coming out of hibernation. Females lay 8 to 40 leathery eggs in June or July, which hatch after about 10 weeks.[1]

Who described it

First described by Lacépède in 1789. The brackets around the authority, (Lacépède, 1789), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

Natrix: the classical Latin word for a water snake, related to the German Natter and English adder[3]

helvetica: ‘of Helvetia (Switzerland)’ – named after where it lives

Grass Snake (helvetica): ‘Grass’ – it lives in grass.
