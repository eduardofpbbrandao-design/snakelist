Queen Snake

Regina septemvittata (Say, 1825)

The Queen Snake is a harmless water snake of eastern North America that eats almost nothing but crayfish. It needs clean, rocky streams, so it disappears where streams become polluted.[1]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Only clean running streams with stony or rocky bottoms, where the water stays at least about 10 °C while it is active.[1]

Diet

A real specialist: it hunts by day and eats crayfish, mostly ones that have just shed their hard shell. It finds them by smelling a chemical they give off while moulting.[1]

Reproduction

Mates in spring or autumn and gives birth to live young. After autumn mating, the female can wait until spring to give birth.[1]

Did you know?

  • It eats almost only soft, freshly moulted crayfish - it can smell when they are moulting.[1]
