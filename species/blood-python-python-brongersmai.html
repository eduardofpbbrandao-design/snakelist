Blood Python

Python brongersmai Stull, 1938

The Blood Python is a short, very thick-bodied, non-venomous python of South-east Asia. Many are a deep red colour. It was once thought to be a kind of Short-tailed Python, but since about 2000 it has been treated as a species of its own.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Very thick, heavy body with a short tail.[1]

Size

Adults: Males are usually about 0.9 to 1.5 m long and females about 1.2 to 1.8 m; a few have reached about 2.4 m. They look heavy for their length.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Peninsular Malaysia, eastern Sumatra and nearby islands, Thailand and Vietnam.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Marshes and swamps in tropical forest, from sea level up to about 650 m.[1]

Behaviour

Mostly active around dawn and dusk.[1]

Reproduction

Lays up to about 30 eggs. The mother coils around them and shivers her muscles to keep them warm.[1]

Did you know?

  • A mother Blood Python can warm her eggs by shivering - a bit like how you shiver when you are cold.[1]

Where the name comes from

Python: named after Python, the serpent that Apollo killed at Delphi in Greek myth[3]

brongersmai: named after Leo Brongersma, a Dutch herpetologist

Blood Python: ‘Python’ – after Python, the giant serpent of Greek myth.
