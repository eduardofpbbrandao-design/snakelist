Brown Tree Snake

Boiga irregularis (Bechstein, 1802)

The Brown Tree Snake is a slender, mildly venomous, rear-fanged tree snake from northern Australia, New Guinea and nearby islands. Accidentally taken to Guam, it became a famous invasive species there.[1,2]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Long, thin body with a large head and cat-like eyes.[1]

Appearance

Slender, with big eyes with vertical pupils like a cat's. Colour varies: brown, greenish or with bands.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Coastal northern and eastern Australia, eastern Indonesia, Papua New Guinea, the Solomon Islands and other islands of Melanesia.[1,2]

Introduced: Guam and some Caroline Islands.[2]

Behaviour

Active at night and lives mainly in trees, hunting by sight and smell.[1]

Diet

Birds, lizards, bats, mice and other rodents; small ones eat frogs and lizards.[1]

Reproduction

Lays 4 to 12 long eggs.[1]

Who described it

First described by Bechstein in 1802. The brackets around the authority, (Bechstein, 1802), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

irregularis: ‘irregular’

Brown Tree Snake: ‘Brown’ – its colour; ‘Tree Snake’ – it lives in trees.
