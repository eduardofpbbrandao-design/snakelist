Massasauga

Sistrurus catenatus (Rafinesque, 1818)

The Eastern Massasauga is a small rattlesnake of eastern North America, around the Great Lakes. It is the only venomous snake in Ontario, Canada.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Small rattlesnake with a rattle on the tail.[1]
  • Large dark blotches down a grey or tan back.[1]

Appearance

Grey or tan with a row of big, rounded dark blotches down the back and smaller spots along the sides. Some are completely black.[1]

Size

Adults: About 60 to 75 cm long.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Southern Ontario in Canada and the Great Lakes region and eastern Midwest of the United States.[1]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Wetlands - swamps, marshes, bogs, fens, wet meadows and floodplains - often using crayfish burrows. In summer some move to drier ground.[1]

Diet

Small animals: mostly voles, mice and shrews for adults, while young ones often eat lizards or snakes; frogs, birds and even centipedes are also taken.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young, usually in August or September - about 5 to 14 at a time. Females usually breed only every second year.[1]

Who described it

First described by Rafinesque in 1818. The brackets around the authority, (Rafinesque, 1818), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

Sistrurus: shares its root with the sistrum, an ancient Egyptian rattle, plus the Greek for tail - for the small rattle on the tail[3]

catenatus: ‘chained’

Worked out from the Latin and Greek roots and the words in the name; parts SnakeList can’t explain are left out.
