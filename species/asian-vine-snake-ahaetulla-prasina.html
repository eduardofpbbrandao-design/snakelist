Asian Vine Snake

Ahaetulla prasina (Boie, 1827)

The Asian Vine Snake is a very slender, often bright green tree snake of South and South-east Asia with a long, pointed snout. It is mildly venomous, with fangs at the back of its mouth.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Very thin, bright green body.[1]

Appearance

Extremely slender with a long, pointed snout more than twice as long as the eye. Light brown to yellow-green, often a startling bright green.[1]

Size

Adults: Up to about 1.8 m long, of which about 0.6 m is tail.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: From north-eastern India and Bangladesh through Myanmar, Thailand and Indochina to southern China, Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia and the Philippines.[1,2]

Did you know?

  • It looks almost exactly like the vine snakes of South America, but they are not close relatives - they evolved the same shape for the same tree-top life.[1]

Who described it

First described by Boie in 1827. The brackets around the authority, (Boie, 1827), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

prasina: ‘leek-green’

Asian Vine Snake: ‘Asian’ – it lives in Asia; ‘Vine Snake’ – it is very thin and green or brown, like a vine.
