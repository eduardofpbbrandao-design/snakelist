Emerald Tree Boa

Corallus caninus (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Emerald Tree Boa is a bright green, tree-dwelling boa of the rainforests of northern South America. It spends almost all its life in the trees and hunts at night. Since 2009 its name has been restricted to the populations of the Guiana Shield, with Amazonian animals treated as a separate species.[1,2]

Appearance

Adults are almost always green, usually with white markings or crossbars along the back. Newborns look completely different: they are usually red-orange, and only rarely green.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Tropical rainforest, where it lives in the trees and stays hidden among the foliage, its colour giving it excellent camouflage.[2,3]

Behaviour

Almost entirely arboreal and active at night. The Guianan Crested Eagle is the only predator known to take it.[1,3]

Diet

Newborns feed on lizards; adults eat birds, rodents and marsupials.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young after a pregnancy of about six to seven months. Litters average about 10 young and can reach about 20.[3]

Who described it

First described by Linnaeus in 1758. The brackets around the authority, (Linnaeus, 1758), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Classification notes

In 2009 Henderson, Passos and Feitosa studied geographic variation in Emerald Tree Boas and revived the name Corallus batesii for the Amazon basin populations, leaving C. caninus for those of the Guiana Shield.[1,4]
