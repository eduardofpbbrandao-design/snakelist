Fer-de-Lance

Bothrops atrox (Linnaeus, 1758)

The Common Lancehead (Fer-de-lance) is a highly venomous pit viper of the tropical lowlands of South America east of the Andes and of Trinidad. It causes many snakebites in its range.[1]

Behaviour

Mostly lives on the ground and is active at night, but swims well and climbs trees when it needs to. It is easily agitated.[1]

Diet

Mostly small mammals such as rodents and opossums, and birds, plus frogs, lizards, snakes, fish, crayfish, centipedes and tarantulas. It bites large prey, lets go and follows its scent.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young; females can sometimes reproduce without a male.[2]

Who described it

First described by Linnaeus in 1758. The brackets around the authority, (Linnaeus, 1758), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

Bothrops: from the Greek bothros (pit) and ops (eye or face) - for the heat-sensing pits on the head[3]

atrox: ‘fierce’

Fer-de-Lance: ‘Fer-de-Lance’ – French for ‘iron of a lance’ (a spearhead), for the shape of its head.
