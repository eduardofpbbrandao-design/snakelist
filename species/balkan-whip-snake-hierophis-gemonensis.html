Balkan Whip Snake

Hierophis gemonensis (Laurenti, 1768)

The Balkan Whip Snake is a fast, slender, harmless snake of the Balkans, Greece and north-eastern Italy.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Slim, fast snake with dark spots near the head.[1]

Appearance

Slender with smooth scales and big eyes with round pupils; olive-grey or yellowish-brown with dark spots towards the front.[1]

Size

Adults: Usually under 1 m, rarely up to 130 cm.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Extreme north-eastern Italy, coastal Slovenia and Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, Serbia, Albania, mainland Greece and many islands including Crete.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Stony places, scree, scrub, open woodland, roadsides, pasture and plantations.[1]

Behaviour

Active by day, mostly on the ground; fast and agile.[1]

Diet

Lizards, big insects such as grasshoppers, nestling birds and small mammals.[1]

Reproduction

Lays 4 to 10 oval eggs, about 30 by 17 mm.[1]

Who described it

First described by Laurenti in 1768. The brackets around the authority, (Laurenti, 1768), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

Hierophis: ‘sacred snake’, from the Greek roots hier- ‘sacred’ + -ophis ‘snake’

gemonensis: ‘from Gemona (Italy)’ (the ending -ensis means ‘from’ a place)

Balkan Whip Snake: ‘Balkan’ – after the Balkans; ‘Whip Snake’ – it is long and thin, like a whip.
