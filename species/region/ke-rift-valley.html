Venomous and non-venomous

Venomous: 20 of 43Non-venomous: 23

16 are listed by SnakeList as dangerous to people: African Cobra Naja pallida, African Rock Python Python sebae, Ashe's Spitting Cobra Naja ashei, Black Mamba Dendroaspis polylepis, Black-necked Spitting Cobra Naja nigricollis, Boomslang Dispholidus typus, Brown Forest Cobra Naja subfulva, Egyptian Saw-scaled Viper Echis pyramidum, Green Night Adder Causus resimus, Kenya Horned Viper Bitis worthingtoni, Lesser African Python Python natalensis, Loveridge's Garter Snake Elapsoidea loveridgei, Peters' Burrowing Asp Atractaspis fallax, Puff Adder Bitis arietans, Rhinoceros Viper (nasicornis) Bitis nasicornis, Variable Burrowing Asp Atractaspis irregularis.

The other 4 venomous species have mild venom: no serious bites to people are documented for them.

Habitats from reviewed species accounts

What SnakeList’s reviewed, sourced accounts say about where each species lives in general, across its range. They do not describe Rift Valley in particular. The other 40 species here have no reviewed account yet, so nothing is said about their habitats.

  • Black Mamba Dendroaspis polylepis: Mostly a ground-dwelling snake of open country: moist and dry savanna, woodland, rocky hills, riverine forest and coastal bush. It hides in holes in the ground, rock crevices, hollow trees, old termite mounds and under tree stumps, and often returns to the same shelter for a long time. Source: Animal Diversity Web, University of Michigan, African Reptiles & Venom.
  • Puff Adder Bitis arietans: Open country such as savanna and grassland. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Sand Boa Eryx colubrinus: Semi-desert, dry scrub savanna and rocky outcrops, wherever there is loose sand or soil to burrow into. Source: Wikipedia.
