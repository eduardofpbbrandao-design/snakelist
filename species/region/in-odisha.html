18 are listed by SnakeList as dangerous to people: Annulated Sea Snake Hydrophis cyanocinctus, Arabian Gulf Sea Snake Hydrophis lapemoides, Banded Krait Bungarus fasciatus, Black-banded Sea Snake Hydrophis nigrocinctus, Cantor's Narrow-headed Sea Snake Hydrophis cantoris, Collared Sea Snake Hydrophis stricticollis, Common Bamboo Viper Craspedocephalus gramineus, Common Seasnake Hydrophis schistosus, Dark Blue-Banded Sea Snake Polyodontognathus caerulescens, Estuarine Sea Snake Hydrophis obscurus, Günther's Sea Snake Microcephalophis cantoris, Indian Rock Python Python molurus, King Cobra Ophiophagus hannah, Monocled Cobra Naja kaouthia, Short Sea Snake Hydrophis curtus, Viperine Sea Snake Hydrophis viperinus, Yellow Sea Snake Hydrophis spiralis, Yellow-bellied Sea Snake Hydrophis platurus.

The other 4 venomous species have mild venom: no serious bites to people are documented for them.

Habitats from reviewed species accounts

What SnakeList’s reviewed, sourced accounts say about where each species lives in general, across its range. They do not describe Odisha in particular. The other 34 species here have no reviewed account yet, so nothing is said about their habitats.

  • King Cobra Ophiophagus hannah: Mainly a forest snake: rainforest, bamboo thickets and mangrove swamps, often near streams and other water, from the lowlands up to about 2,000 m. It also turns up in plantations and farmland next to forest. Source: Young People's Trust for the Environment, Smithsonian's National Zoo.
  • Yellow-bellied Sea Snake Hydrophis platurus: Completely pelagic (living in the open ocean). It is often found along slicks and drift lines - the lines where surface currents meet and floating debris collects - and it is the only air-breathing marine predator known to hunt at these surface features. Source: Wikipedia.
