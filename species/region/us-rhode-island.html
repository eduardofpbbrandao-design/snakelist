Habitats from reviewed species accounts

What SnakeList’s reviewed, sourced accounts say about where each species lives in general, across its range. They do not describe Rhode Island in particular. The other 1 species here has no reviewed account yet, so nothing is said about its habitat.

  • Brown Snake Storeria dekayi: Most kinds of wet and dry habitats east of the Great Plains, from sea level to about 1,400 m. It likes moist places and is most often found near people - in cities more than in the countryside. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Common Gartersnake Thamnophis sirtalis: Usually close to water: along rivers, lakes and reservoirs, in wetlands and in the strips of plants beside streams. It also turns up at forest edges and even in vacant lots in towns, but is rarely found in dry places far from water. Source: South Dakota Amphibians and Reptiles, Illinois Natural History Survey.
  • Common Wormsnake Carphophis amoenus: Moist, rocky woodland, under rotting logs and stumps. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Eastern Hognose Snake Heterodon platirhinos: Open areas with sandy, loose soils. Loose soil is important for nesting and egg-laying, and for digging out prey. Source: Government of Canada, Species at Risk Public Registry, Wikipedia.
  • Eastern Rat Snake Pantherophis alleghaniensis: Almost any place rich in rodents and with somewhere to climb: forests, woodland edges, fields, swamps and farm buildings. Source: Gopher Tortoise Council, Wikipedia.
  • Eastern Ribbonsnake Thamnophis saurita: Along ponds, streams, swamps and wet woods or grassland. It uses shallow water to hunt and to escape. In winter it shelters underground, for example under rock piles. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Milk Snake Lampropeltis triangulum: Rocky hillsides, open woodland, woodland edges, clearings and old fields, and also farm buildings and rock outcrops. It spends winter in rock crevices, mammal burrows or building foundations. Source: The Canadian Encyclopedia, Maine Department of Inland Fisheries and Wildlife.
  • Racer Coluber constrictor: Usually open, grassy places, but also brush, swamps, roadsides and suburbs; in Florida it is the commonest snake in neighbourhoods. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Redbelly Snake Storeria occipitomaculata: Woodlands; it hides under logs and leaves. In the north it uses old ant mounds, which stay warm in the sun. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Ringneck Snake Diadophis punctatus: Many habitats, wherever there is plenty of cover: open woodland near rocky hillsides, forests, damp places with fallen wood, and streamside areas in dry regions. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Smooth Green Snake Opheodrys vernalis: Marshes, meadows, open woods and the edges of streams. Source: Wikipedia.
