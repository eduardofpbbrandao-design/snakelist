Habitats from reviewed species accounts

What SnakeList’s reviewed, sourced accounts say about where each species lives in general, across its range. They do not describe California in particular. The other 28 species here have no reviewed account yet, so nothing is said about their habitats.

  • California Kingsnake Lampropeltis californiae: Very adaptable: woodland, chaparral, grassland, desert, farmland and suburban areas, often near rocks or streams. Source: Wikipedia, CaliforniaHerps.com.
  • Checkered Garter Snake Thamnophis marcianus: Desert, grassland, shrubland and forest, usually close to water, up to about 2,200 m. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Coachwhip Masticophis flagellum: Open, sandy places: pine woods, old fields, prairies, sandhill scrub and coastal dunes. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Common Gartersnake Thamnophis sirtalis: Usually close to water: along rivers, lakes and reservoirs, in wetlands and in the strips of plants beside streams. It also turns up at forest edges and even in vacant lots in towns, but is rarely found in dry places far from water. Source: South Dakota Amphibians and Reptiles, Illinois Natural History Survey.
  • Common Kingsnake Lampropeltis getula: Uses a wide range of habitats, from pine woods and hardwood forest to the edges of swamps, fields and farmland. Source: Florida Museum of Natural History, Wikipedia.
  • Glossy Snake Arizona elegans: Semi-dry grassland. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Ground Snake (semiannulata) Sonora semiannulata: Dry, rocky places with loose soil. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Longnose Snake Rhinocheilus lecontei: Desert, grassland, shrubland and savanna. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Mojave Rattlesnake Crotalus scutulatus: Broad desert valleys and lower mountain slopes with sparse plants such as creosote bush, mesquite, cacti and Joshua trees; it tends to avoid thick vegetation and very rocky ground. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Northern Rubber Boa Charina bottae: Grassland, meadows, chaparral, forests and high mountains, from sea level to over 3,000 m. It cannot stand heat and dryness but copes with surprisingly cold places. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Northwestern Garter Snake Thamnophis ordinoides: Sunny meadow edges surrounded by forest; in British Columbia also near fresh and salt water. On hot days it hides under stumps and rocks. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Racer Coluber constrictor: Usually open, grassy places, but also brush, swamps, roadsides and suburbs; in Florida it is the commonest snake in neighbourhoods. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Red Diamond Rattlesnake Crotalus ruber: Dense chaparral in the foothills, cactus patches and brushy boulders, from sea level to about 1,500 m. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Ringneck Snake Diadophis punctatus: Many habitats, wherever there is plenty of cover: open woodland near rocky hillsides, forests, damp places with fallen wood, and streamside areas in dry regions. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Rosy Boa Lichanura trivirgata: Rocky deserts - most often granite outcrops. Where there are few rocks it hides in rodent burrows. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Sidewinder Crotalus cerastes: Sandy flats with creosote bush and mesquite, and occasionally rocky slopes. Source: U.S. National Park Service, Death Valley.
  • Southwestern Speckled Rattlesnake Crotalus pyrrhus: Rocky hillsides, canyons, slopes and ledges. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Striped Racer Masticophis lateralis: Coast, foothills and chaparral. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Striped Whipsnake Masticophis taeniatus: Shrubland, grassland, sagebrush flats, canyons, piñon-juniper woodland and open pine-oak forest, often near rocky streams. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Two-striped Garter Snake Thamnophis hammondii: Next to permanent or seasonal water. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Western Diamond-backed Rattlesnake Crotalus atrox: Very adaptable. It lives in deserts, grasslands, scrubland, rocky canyons and even pine-oak forest, from coastal lowlands up to about 2,100 m (7,000 ft). It shelters in rock crevices and in burrows dug by mammals. Source: Animal Diversity Web, University of Michigan, Pajarito Environmental Education Center (PEEC) Nature.
  • Western Patchnose Snake Salvadora hexalepis: Dry deserts. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Western Terrestrial Garter Snake Thamnophis elegans: Grasslands, woodlands and conifer forests from sea level up to about 3,960 m. Mostly a land snake, but some mountain populations live partly in water. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Yellow-bellied Sea Snake Hydrophis platurus: Completely pelagic (living in the open ocean). It is often found along slicks and drift lines - the lines where surface currents meet and floating debris collects - and it is the only air-breathing marine predator known to hunt at these surface features. Source: Wikipedia.
