The other 6 venomous species have mild venom: no serious bites to people are documented for them.

Habitats from reviewed species accounts

What SnakeList’s reviewed, sourced accounts say about where each species lives in general, across its range. They do not describe Coast in particular. The other 44 species here have no reviewed account yet, so nothing is said about their habitats.

  • Black Mamba Dendroaspis polylepis: Mostly a ground-dwelling snake of open country: moist and dry savanna, woodland, rocky hills, riverine forest and coastal bush. It hides in holes in the ground, rock crevices, hollow trees, old termite mounds and under tree stumps, and often returns to the same shelter for a long time. Source: Animal Diversity Web, University of Michigan, African Reptiles & Venom.
  • Green Mamba Dendroaspis angusticeps: Coastal forest and bush, dune forest and some montane forest. It favours dense, shady vegetation and is seldom found in open country. Source: Wikipedia, African Snakebite Institute.
  • Puff Adder Bitis arietans: Open country such as savanna and grassland. Source: Wikipedia.
  • Sand Boa Eryx colubrinus: Semi-desert, dry scrub savanna and rocky outcrops, wherever there is loose sand or soil to burrow into. Source: Wikipedia.
