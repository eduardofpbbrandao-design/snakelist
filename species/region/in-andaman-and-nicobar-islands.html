The other 3 venomous species have mild venom: no serious bites to people are documented for them.

Habitats from reviewed species accounts

What SnakeList’s reviewed, sourced accounts say about where each species lives in general, across its range. They do not describe the Andaman and Nicobar Islands in particular. The other 31 species here have no reviewed account yet, so nothing is said about their habitats.

  • Common Krait Bungarus caeruleus: A wide range of lowland habitats, including fields, scrub and villages. It shelters in termite mounds, rat holes, brick piles and similar hiding places, and regularly enters houses. Source: Wikipedia, Journal of Herpetology 54(1).
  • King Cobra Ophiophagus hannah: Mainly a forest snake: rainforest, bamboo thickets and mangrove swamps, often near streams and other water, from the lowlands up to about 2,000 m. It also turns up in plantations and farmland next to forest. Source: Young People's Trust for the Environment, Smithsonian's National Zoo.
  • Reticulated Python Malayopython reticulatus: Tropical forest, usually near rivers and other water. Source: Encyclopaedia Britannica, Active Wild.
  • Yellow-bellied Sea Snake Hydrophis platurus: Completely pelagic (living in the open ocean). It is often found along slicks and drift lines - the lines where surface currents meet and floating debris collects - and it is the only air-breathing marine predator known to hunt at these surface features. Source: Wikipedia.
