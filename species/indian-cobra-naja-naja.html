Indian Cobra

Naja naja

The Indian Cobra, or Spectacled Cobra, is the classic cobra of the Indian subcontinent. It is one of the "Big Four" snakes that cause most of the serious snakebites in India and Sri Lanka, partly because it often lives close to people.[1,2]

Identification

Appearance

When alarmed it raises the front of its body and spreads a broad hood. On the back of the hood there is usually a mark of two rings joined by a curved line, like a pair of spectacles - though a few snakes lack it.[1]

Size

Adults: Can grow to around 2 m. Some specimens, particularly from Sri Lanka, reach 2.1 to 2.2 m, but that is uncommon.[3,1]

Range

Published distribution

Native: The Indian subcontinent, including India and Sri Lanka.[1]

Behaviour

Spreading the hood is a defensive display meant to startle an attacker so that the cobra can escape.[1]

Diet

Mainly rodents, lizards and frogs.[1]
