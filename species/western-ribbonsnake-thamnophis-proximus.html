Western Ribbonsnake

Thamnophis proximus (Say, 1823)

The Western Ribbon Snake is a slender, harmless garter snake that lives near water, from the central United States through Mexico to Costa Rica.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Three light stripes on a dark, very slim body.[1]

Appearance

Very slender with a long tail (about a third of its length). Black, brown or olive with three light stripes; the lips are white and unmarked, standing out against the dark head.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: The central and southern United States, eastern Mexico and Central America south to central Costa Rica.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Many habitats, usually not far from water, from sea level to nearly 2,500 m.[1]

Diet

Small animals: earthworms, crayfish, lizards, fish, frogs and tadpoles.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to live young.[2]

Who described it

First described by Say in 1823. The brackets around the authority, (Say, 1823), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

Thamnophis: from the Greek thamnos (bush or shrub) and ophis (snake)[3]

proximus: ‘nearest’

Western Ribbonsnake: ‘Western’ – where it lives (in the west).
