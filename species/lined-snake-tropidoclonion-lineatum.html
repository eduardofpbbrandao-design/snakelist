Lined Snake

Tropidoclonion lineatum (Hallowell, 1856)

The Lined Snake is a small, harmless snake of the central United States. It is the only species in its genus.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Light stripe down the middle of the back.[1]

Appearance

Olive-green to brown with a tan or yellow stripe down the back and one along each side; the belly has two rows of neat black half-moon spots.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: The central United States from Illinois to Colorado and Texas.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Savanna, shrubland and grassland with soft, moist soil - also backyards, vacant lots and dumps.[1]

Behaviour

Spends most of its time hidden under rocks, leaves and logs or in the soil.[1]

Diet

Mostly earthworms.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to about seven or eight live young in August, each 10 to 12 cm long.[1]
