Green Tree Snake

Dendrelaphis punctulatus (Gray, 1826)

The Common (Green) Tree Snake is a slender, large-eyed, harmless snake of northern and eastern Australia and New Guinea. It is agile, climbs well and is a strong swimmer.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Very slender body with big eyes.[1]
  • Active by day, often in trees.[1]

Size

Adults: In a Queensland study, the body (without the tail) averaged about 1 m in females and 0.93 m in males. The total length can reach about 1.6 m, and some sources say up to 2 m.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Northern and eastern Australia - from the Kimberley across to Cape York and the Torres Strait and down the east coast into New South Wales - and Papua New Guinea.[1,2]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Bushland, well-vegetated banks of rivers and creeks, rainforest edges and gardens with ponds.[1]

Behaviour

Active by day; it rests at night in hollow trees, logs, leaves or rock cracks. It is harmless to people but may bite or give off a smell if handled.[1]

Diet

Frogs, water skinks and other small reptiles and their eggs, small fish, small mammals and turtle hatchlings.[1]

Reproduction

Lays 5 to 12 long eggs.[1]

Who described it

First described by Gray in 1826. The brackets around the authority, (Gray, 1826), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

punctulatus: ‘finely dotted’

Green Tree Snake: ‘Green’ – its colour; ‘Tree Snake’ – it lives in trees.
