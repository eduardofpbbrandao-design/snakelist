Southern Water Snake

Nerodia fasciata (Linnaeus, 1766)

The Banded (or Southern) Water Snake is a harmless, mostly aquatic snake of the south-eastern United States.[1]

Identification

How to recognise it

  • Dark crossbands on a grey or brown body.[1]
  • Usually in or near fresh water.[1]

Appearance

Grey, greenish-grey or brown with dark crossbands, a flat head and a fairly heavy body; many are so dark the pattern hardly shows. The belly is off-white.[1]

Size

Adults: Usually 56 to 107 cm long; the record, a Florida snake, was about 159 cm (62.5 in).[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: From southern Illinois south to Louisiana and east to North Carolina and Florida.[1,2]

Introduced: Introduced populations live in California (over 300 were caught around Los Angeles in 2009) and other places.[1]

Behaviour and ecology

Habitat

Almost any fresh water - lakes, marshes, ponds and streams - staying among plants along the shore.[1]

Behaviour

Active by day and by night; it basks on logs and branches over the water or forages in the shallows.[1]

Diet

Mainly fish and frogs; young ones eat mostly fish and switch to frogs as they grow.[1]

Reproduction

Gives birth to about 15 to 20 live young in summer.[1]

Where the name comes from

fasciata: ‘banded’

Southern Water Snake: ‘Southern’ – where it lives (in the south); ‘Water Snake’ – it lives in or near water.
