Japanese Four-lined Ratsnake

Elaphe quadrivirgata (Boie, 1826)

The Japanese Four-lined Ratsnake ('shimahebi', the striped snake) is a non-venomous snake found all over Japan except the Ryukyu Islands.[1]

Identification

Appearance

Yellow or light brown with four black stripes along the body. All-black ones also exist - in Japan they are called 'crow snakes'.[1]

Size

Adults: About 1 to 1.5 m long.[1]

Distribution

Published distribution

Native: Japan from Hokkaido to Kyushu and nearby islands, and Kunashir Island.[1,2]

Who described it

First described by Boie in 1826. The brackets around the authority, (Boie, 1826), mean it was first described in a different genus.

Where the name comes from

quadrivirgata: ‘four-striped’, from Latin roots: quadri- ‘four’ + -virgata ‘striped’

Japanese Four-lined Ratsnake: ‘Japanese’ – it lives in Japan; ‘Four-lined’ – for its four lines; ‘Ratsnake’ – it eats rats and other rodents.

Worked out from the Latin and Greek roots and the words in the name; parts SnakeList can’t explain are left out.
